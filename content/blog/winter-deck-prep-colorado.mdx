**Twenty minutes with a broom and a screwdriver in October will do more for your deck than any spring project — because winter doesn't create deck problems, it accelerates the ones already there.** Here's the checklist, plus the two winter habits (metal shovels and the wrong ice melt) that quietly destroy decks that were fine in September.

## The 20-minute fall checklist

1. **Clear the gaps between boards.** The gaps are the deck's drainage system. Leaves and pine needles pack in, water ponds instead of draining, and every freeze wedges the boards a little harder. A putty knife or a stiff broom run along the gaps clears them.
2. **Sweep the ledger line.** Where the deck meets the house is the most consequential joint on the structure. Debris piled there holds moisture against the connection all winter. Sweep it bare.
3. **Check the flashing before the first freeze.** Look at the metal flashing along the ledger. Lifted edges, gaps, or rust mean water is getting behind it — and water behind the ledger is how decks fail seriously. Our [deck safety inspection guide](/deck-safety-inspection-guide) shows what healthy flashing looks like; if yours doesn't match, that's a fall repair, not a spring one.
4. **Re-seat proud fasteners.** Walk the deck and look for screw heads sitting above the surface — freeze-thaw backs them out a little more each year. Drive them home. A proud fastener under snow is a toe-catcher you can't see.
5. **Shake the railing.** Grab every post and pull. Ice on the stairs is exactly when someone grabs the rail hard and expects it to hold — code expects a 200-pound load in any direction, and so should you. A wobbly post is a [quick fall fix](/services/deck-repair); a failed one in January is an emergency.
6. **Evict the firewood.** Firewood stacked under the deck holds moisture against posts and joists and invites the insects that come with it. Store it somewhere the deck isn't.
7. **Look underneath.** Two minutes with a flashlight: dark streaks, soft wood at post bases, white mineral tracks on hardware. Anything suspicious, photograph it and get it looked at while crews still have fall room.
8. **Book repairs now, not in April.** Anything the checklist turned up is cheapest and fastest to fix in fall — see below.

## What does winter actually do to an unprepared deck?

| Winter force | What it attacks | How it plays out |
|---|---|---|
| Freeze-thaw cycling | Fasteners and connections | Water in board gaps and screw holes freezes and expands — dozens of cycles a season — backing fasteners out and working joints loose |
| Snow load | Weak or aging framing | A wet spring dump is heavy; framing with hidden rot or undersized spans sags or worse under weight it once shrugged off |
| Ice melt (calcium chloride) | Metal hardware below | Salt brine drips through the gaps onto joist hangers and connectors and corrodes the steel holding the deck together |
| Trapped debris | The wood itself | Packed leaves hold moisture against boards and the ledger all winter — rot's ideal setup |

None of these forces breaks a sound, clean deck in one winter. All of them find whatever weakness fall left in place, and compound it.

## How should you handle snow on a deck?

Three rules cover it:

**Plastic shovel, never metal.** A metal blade gouges wood and slices composite capping. Plastic clears the same snow without the scars.

**Shovel along the boards, not across.** Run the shovel parallel to the decking so the blade rides the boards instead of catching every edge crossways. And don't scrape to bare wood — leave a thin layer and let the sun finish the job; it usually does within a day or two here.

**Skip the ice melt if there's metal below — use sand.** This is the one almost nobody knows. Calcium chloride and its cousins run through the board gaps as brine and sit on the joist hangers, post connectors, and fasteners underneath — and salt corrodes exactly the hardware the whole structure hangs on. Plain sand gives traction with zero chemistry. (This matters less on a [covered deck](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), which is a genuine part of the case for a roof — the snow never lands.)

You don't have to clear every storm. Most Colorado snowfalls sublimate off a deck in days. Clear the big, wet spring dumps — especially on an older deck whose framing you're less sure of.

## Why fall beats spring for repairs

Everything the checklist finds is easier to fix in October than in April, for three unglamorous reasons. Crews have room — fall is when the summer crush ends, which is the same reason [fall and winter are the smart season to build](/blog/best-time-to-build-deck-colorado). The ground is still workable, so anything involving a post or footing happens before frost locks the soil. And the problem is still small — the loose post that's a $200 fall fix has spent a winter of freeze-thaw becoming a bigger one by March, when it joins everyone else's March discoveries on a waiting list.

If the checklist turns up something beyond a screwdriver's pay grade — soft framing, failing flashing, a surface that's [cupping and splitting its way out](/services/deck-replacement) — fall is also the right time for the bigger conversation, while there's calendar room to actually act on it.
