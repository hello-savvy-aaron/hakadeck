**Replacing a deck railing in the Denver metro runs $30–$150+ per linear foot installed depending on the system — most decks land in the $30–$90 band, which puts a full replacement on a typical mid-size deck at $2,000–$8,000+.** The deck stays; the railing goes. Here's the 2026 math, and the code your new rail has to meet.

## What does railing replacement cost per foot?

| Railing type | Installed cost per linear foot | Why you'd pick it |
|---|---|---|
| Composite / basic metal | $30–$60 | The value workhorse; matches a composite deck |
| Aluminum (powder-coated) | $60–$90 | Slim, no-maintenance, holds up in our sun |
| Cable rail (stainless) | $80–$130 | Gets the pickets out of a view |
| Glass panel | $100–$150+ | Maximum view, wind block |

A mid-size deck carries **60–70 linear feet** of railing once you run the perimeter and the stairs. Multiply through and the project brackets itself: composite or basic metal lands **$2,000–$4,000**; aluminum around **$4,000–$6,000**; cable and glass push **$5,000–$8,000+**. Stairs price above the flat runs — every piece cuts on an angle — a point we cover in our [stairs and railings cost guide](/blog/deck-stairs-railings-cost-guide).

## Why do Denver deck railings fail in the first place?

Three reasons, and only one of them is age.

**Freeze-thaw works the connections loose.** Colorado's daily winter swing across the freezing line expands and contracts every joint on the deck, and the railing posts — tall levers bolted to the rim — feel it first. A post that moved a sixteenth of an inch a hundred cycles ago wobbles an inch today.

**Altitude UV eats wood infill.** At a mile up, UV runs roughly 25% stronger than sea level. Wood pickets bleach, check, and split years before the deck boards under your feet give up, which is why so many otherwise-sound decks wear a gray, splintering rail.

**Code drift.** Plenty of older metro decks were built to rules that no longer exist — 32-inch rail heights, picket gaps over 4 inches, 2x2s toenailed to the rim. The deck was legal when it went in. The replacement rail won't be legal unless it meets today's numbers.

## What code does a replacement railing have to meet?

A new railing doesn't get grandfathered — it meets current code, full stop:

- **36 inches minimum height** wherever the deck surface is 30 inches or more above grade — the same threshold that makes a guardrail required at all.
- **The 4-inch sphere rule.** No opening in the infill — between pickets, under the bottom rail, at the stair transitions — can pass a 4-inch sphere.
- **200-pound load, any direction.** The top rail has to resist a concentrated 200-pound push outward, inward, or down. This is the number that separates a railing from a decoration, and it's carried entirely by the posts and their connections.

Every one of these is on the checklist in our [permits and code guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro), and inspectors check all of them when a railing is part of a permitted job.

## Which railing material is worth it?

| Priority | Best pick | The trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Budget, match the deck | Composite | Chunkier profile, some sightline lost |
| Zero maintenance | Aluminum | Costs more up front, then nothing forever |
| The view | Cable or glass | The premium tier; cable needs occasional re-tensioning, glass needs cleaning |
| Warm wood look | Wood | Refinishing every 2–3 years in our UV — the maintenance you were likely escaping |

If your deck looks at the foothills or open space, cable and slim-profile aluminum exist to get the rail out of the sightline — you're paying for the view, not the metal. If the old wood rail is why you're here, buying wood again just restarts the clock. We walk through how each system ages in Colorado on our [railings page](/services/railings).

## What's actually involved in a railing swap?

Less than a rebuild, more than a hardware run. The old rail comes off, the posts and their connections get evaluated, and here's the honest part: **the posts matter more than the infill**. A beautiful new railing on loose posts is furniture — it looks right and fails the 200-pound test. A proper replacement means posts through-bolted with structural hardware into sound framing, blocking added where the old build skipped it, and then the rail system the manufacturer's testing was based on. Done right, it's a **one-to-two crew-day job** on most decks, and it's covered under [our workmanship warranty](/warranty) like any build.

## Does a railing swap need a permit?

Usually not — a like-for-like railing replacement is typically treated as repair, not construction. But every south-metro city draws that line slightly differently, so we confirm with the local jurisdiction before we start rather than guessing. Where the job grows structural — new posts, sistered framing, rim repair — a permit often does enter the picture, and we [handle that process](/process) end to end.

## The honest caveat: when it's not a railing job

If a post wobbles because the rim joist it bolts to is soft, punky, or pulling off the house, no railing fixes that — that's a repair conversation first. Run our [10-minute safety inspection](/deck-safety-inspection-guide) before you shop railings: probe the rim, check the ledger, look at the post bases. If something's off, [deck repair](/services/deck-repair) comes first and the railing rides along on the same mobilization — which is cheaper than doing them a year apart anyway.
