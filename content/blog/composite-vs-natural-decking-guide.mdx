---Picking a decking material is the single biggest decision in any deck project. The surface you choose sets the installed rate — from **$20–35 per square foot** for pressure-treated pine to **$50–85** for PVC — dictates how many weekends you lose to maintenance, and determines whether the deck still looks good when you sell the house. Here are the numbers, the trade-offs, and a decision tree to land on the right material for *your* project.

## The Two Camps

**Natural wood** covers pressure-treated pine, cedar, redwood, and tropical hardwoods like Ipe and Cumaru. It's the traditional choice — real grain, real feel, lower entry cost.

**Composite** is a manufactured board made from wood fibers and recycled plastic, wrapped in a protective polymer "cap." Modern PVC and mineral-based boards take it a step further with no wood content at all. The pitch: wood's look, plastic's lifespan.

## What It Actually Costs

Denver-metro 2026 ranges, material plus professional installation. Composite boards alone run **$8–14 per square foot**; labor adds **$15–35 per square foot** — roughly 40–60% of the finished total, whatever you walk on.

| Material | Installed / sq ft | Lifespan | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure-treated pine | $20–$35 | 10–15 yrs | Stain/seal every 2–3 yrs |
| Cedar | $25–$40 | 15–20 yrs | Stain/seal every 2–3 yrs |
| Redwood | $30–$45 | 20–25 yrs | Stain/seal every 2–3 yrs |
| Tropical hardwood (Ipe, Cumaru) | $45–$75 | 25–50 yrs | Oil annually (or let it gray) |
| Capped composite (Trex, TimberTech, Fiberon) | $40–$70 | 25–30 yrs | Wash 1–2x per year |
| Mineral-based composite (Deckorators) | $40–$70 | 30+ yrs | Wash 1–2x per year |
| PVC / cellular vinyl (AZEK, Wolf) | $50–$85 | 30–50 yrs | Wash 1–2x per year |
| Aluminum (Wahoo, AridDek) | $50–$90 | 50+ yrs | Rinse only |

For a typical 16×20 (320 sq ft) deck with railing, that's roughly **$6,500–11,000** in pressure-treated, **$13,000–25,000** in capped composite, and **$16,000–27,000** in PVC. For calibration: the 2025 Zonda Cost vs Value report puts the national-average 16×20 composite deck addition at about **$25,000** (wood about **$18,000**) — with wood recouping ~82% at resale and composite ~68%. The full size-by-size breakdown is in our [deck cost guide](/deck-cost-guide-denver).

## The 10-Year Math

Sticker price isn't the whole story. A 320 sq ft pressure-treated deck at roughly **$9,000** wants a clean-and-restain every 2–3 years — **$400–900 per round** hired out — plus board and fastener repairs as UV and freeze-thaw do their work. Call it **$3,000–5,000 over ten years**, on a surface that's aging anyway. The same deck in capped composite at ~$16,000 needs soap and water. The day-one gap narrows to a few thousand dollars by year ten — and past year ten, composite is simply ahead.

## The Colorado Factor

The comparison tilts harder toward low-maintenance materials here than in most markets. At the south metro's **5,800–6,200 feet**, UV runs about **25% stronger** than at sea level — which is why a wood deck rated "re-stain every 3 years" realistically needs attention every **18–24 months** on the Front Range. Add **35–90 freeze-thaw cycles a year** working water into every screw hole and end grain, and wood's maintenance clock ticks faster than the national averages suggest. The [full Colorado materials guide](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) goes deeper.

## What's New in 2026

**Cooler-surface technology.** A standard dark composite board in direct Colorado sun can hit **~160°F on a 90°F day**. TimberTech's CoolDeck capstock and similar tech from Trex (Transcend Lineage) now run **30–35°F cooler** than older dark composites — worth the upgrade for dark tones on a south- or west-facing deck.

**Mineral-based composites.** Deckorators Voyage and Vault replace wood flour with a mineral core — lighter, stronger, no moisture uptake, and rated for ground-contact installs most composites can't handle.

**Higher recycled content.** Trex boards are roughly **95% recycled material**. For sustainability-minded clients, that often outranks tropical hardwoods, which carry deforestation concerns even when FSC-certified.

**Realistic grain.** Four-sided embossing and multi-tonal streaking have closed most of the visual gap. From six feet away, premium capped composite is genuinely hard to distinguish from stained hardwood.

**Hidden fasteners as default.** Almost every composite line ships with a grooved-edge clip system now — no screw heads, and no exposed fastener holes for freeze-thaw to work on.

## Decision Tree

Run through these in order. Stop at the first one that fits.

**1. Is your total budget under $12,000 for a standard 300–400 sq ft deck?**
Pressure-treated pine at **$20–35 per square foot**. Plan to re-stain it every other year. It's the honest answer.

**2. Do you want real wood grain and you're willing to maintain it?**
Cedar or redwood for the balance of looks and cost. Ipe or Cumaru if you want a 30-year deck and don't mind the price or the weight.

**3. Will you sell the house in the next 5–7 years?**
Entry capped composite (Trex Enhance, TimberTech EDGE, Fiberon Good Life). Strong resale appeal, zero maintenance during your ownership, bottom of the $40–70 range.

**4. Forever home, or you hate yard work?**
Premium PVC (TimberTech AZEK, Wolf) or mineral-based composite. Highest upfront cost, lowest lifetime cost, warranties from 50 years to lifetime.

**5. Full sun and you want dark colors?**
Specifically request a cool-surface capstock line. Don't let anyone talk you into a standard dark composite for a south-facing deck — that's the 160°F board.

**6. Coastal, fire-zone, or rooftop project?**
Aluminum. It costs more and it's still the right tool.

## A Few Honest Trade-offs

Composite isn't perfect. It scratches (especially older uncapped lines), dark colors get hot without cool-surface tech, and you can't sand and refinish it the way you can wood. If a board fails in year 18, you're matching a discontinued color.

Wood isn't doomed either. A well-maintained Ipe deck will outlast most composites, and no manufactured board matches weathered cedar or freshly-oiled hardwood. If you enjoy spring deck-prep weekends, wood rewards the effort.

## The Short Version

Tight budget, short horizon: pressure-treated pine. Want real wood and don't mind upkeep: [cedar or hardwood](/services/hardwood-cedar-decks). Want to install once and forget it: [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) or PVC, with a cool-surface capstock if the deck bakes in the sun.
