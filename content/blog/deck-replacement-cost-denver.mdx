If your deck went up in the '80s or '90s — and across Centennial, Littleton, and the rest of the south metro, a huge share did — you're not researching a luxury. You're pricing overdue maintenance on the most-used room of the house. Here's what deck replacement actually costs [across the Denver metro](/locations/denver) in 2026, and the one question that swings the price by tens of thousands of dollars.

## The one question: replace, or re-deck?

Two very different projects hide under the phrase "deck replacement":

- **Re-decking (resurfacing).** The framing underneath is sound, so the old boards and railings come off and a new surface goes on over the structure you already own. Typical Denver-metro range: **$20–$35 per square foot installed** for capped composite.
- **Full replacement.** Teardown to grade, haul-off, new engineered footings and framing, new surface and code-current railings. This is new-deck pricing plus demolition: **$40–$70 per square foot** for composite, **plus roughly $1,500–$3,500** for teardown and disposal, depending on size and access.

Run against the three most common deck sizes, that's:

| Size | Re-deck (sound framing) | Full replacement (with tear-out) |
|---|---|---|
| 12×16 (192 sq ft) | $3,800–$6,700 | $9,500–$16,500 |
| 16×20 (320 sq ft) | $6,400–$11,200 | $14,500–$28,500 |
| 20×24 (480 sq ft) | $9,600–$16,800 | $20,500–$40,500 |

On the average 16×20, the framing verdict is worth roughly **$8,000–$17,000** — and it isn't a preference, it's a structural fact. That's why every replacement quote we give starts with a [real inspection](/services/deck-repair): ledger, footings, posts, joists, hardware. If the bones are good, we say so and quote the cheaper job.

## How to tell which job your deck needs

The short version of the inspection, in decision-rule form:

- **Re-deck qualifies** when joists pass the screwdriver test (a blade that sinks in means rot), the ledger is bolted and flashed, footings sit at frost depth, and the hardware is surface-rusted at worst.
- **Replacement wins** when rot has reached more than one or two framing members, the ledger is nailed or unflashed, or footings were poured shallow and have heaved — common on pre-code builds.
- **The 16-inch catch.** Most composite boards require joists at 16 inches on center (12 inches for diagonal layouts). Plenty of older wood decks were framed at 24 inches — sometimes sistering new joists in fixes it, but past a point the "re-deck" quietly becomes a rebuild and honest pricing should say so.

## What pushes a replacement toward the top of the range

- **Height.** Walk-out and second-story decks carry taller posts, bigger footings, and engineered railing loads. Expect elevated builds to run **20–40% above** a ground-level deck of the same size — our [second-story deck cost guide](/blog/second-story-deck-cost-colorado) breaks this down.
- **What the old deck is hiding.** Rotted rim joists, an unflashed ledger, or footings poured shallow of frost depth get discovered at teardown, not before. A builder who inspected first will have warned you; one who bid off a photo will change-order you.
- **Code, since your last deck.** Today's rules: guardrails required above 30 inches of height, at 36 inches minimum, with no opening that passes a 4-inch sphere; stair rises capped at 7¾ inches; footings at 30–36 inches for Front Range frost depth. A replacement brings all of it current — that's a feature, and it's [permitted and inspected](/blog/deck-permits-centennial-arapahoe-douglas) like one, typically **$150–$500 in permit fees** and three inspections (footing holes, rough framing, final).
- **Add-ons.** Railing runs **$30–$90 per linear foot installed** and stairs **$150–$400 per step** — same as on a new build. Our [stairs and railings cost guide](/blog/deck-stairs-railings-cost-guide) has the full numbers.

## What an honest replacement bid looks like

For a common project — a worn 16×20 wood deck replaced with capped composite, mid-height, 36 linear feet of aluminum railing, four steps — an itemized quote lands around **$18,800**: roughly $900 for plans and permit, $2,000 demo and haul-off, $2,600 footings and posts, $4,900 framing, $4,300 decking, $2,300 railing, $1,100 stairs, $700 lighting rough-in. The full line-by-line version is in our [deck cost guide](/deck-cost-guide-denver). If a bid you're holding can't be broken into lines like those, you can't compare it to anything.

Timeline runs shorter than most people expect: 1–3 weeks for the permit in most south-metro cities, then a one-to-two-week build from tear-out to final inspection for a typical single-level replacement. Plan on four to six weeks door to door — which is why the smart replacements get signed in winter and built before the first barbecue.

## Is replacement worth it?

Against the alternative — annual patch-and-stain triage on a deck that's structurally done — almost always. A pressure-treated deck wants a **$400–$900** clean-and-restain every 2–3 years plus board and fastener repairs; call it **$3,000–$5,000 per decade** to maintain a surface that's aging anyway. A capped-composite rebuild resets the clock by decades on soap-and-water upkeep, carries 25–50 year fade-and-stain warranties, and reads as a straight asset on an inspection report instead of a red flag. For calibration, the 2025 Zonda Cost vs Value report puts a national-average 16×20 composite deck addition around **$25,000 with ~68% recouped at resale**. And unlike the original builder-grade deck, a replacement is designed for how you actually use the space, not for the cheapest possible line on a spec sheet.
