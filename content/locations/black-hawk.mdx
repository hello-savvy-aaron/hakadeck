## A casino town in a steep gulch

Black Hawk is better known for its casinos than its houses, and with about 130 residents there are not many of the latter. What exists is distinctive: a handful of relocated Victorian homes on Chase Gulch and Dory Hill above the Gregory Street core, and newer mountain homes on large forested lots up Highway 119 toward the Peak to Peak. At 8,042 feet, nearly all of it sits on steep, rocky ground where the buildable envelope is smaller than the acreage suggests. That mix — altitude, slope, rock, forest — is the design brief for every deck here.

## Permits: SAFEbuilt, through CommunityCore

The City of Black Hawk Community Planning & Development Building Division issues the permit; SAFEbuilt has run the program since 2015, with every permit pulled through the CommunityCore portal. We handle contractor registration, the historic-district commission packet where it applies, and inspections through final. Unincorporated parcels outside city limits answer to [Gilpin County](/locations/gilpin-county) instead, and we confirm which side of the line you're on first.

## Forty-eight inches of frost, and Front Range wind

Black Hawk's adopted design criteria, under the 2021 I-Codes, put frost depth at 48 inches — well below the 30-to-36-inch line most of the metro uses. Every footing we pour here goes below it. The city lists a 77 psf snow load for commercial work and keeps residential design criteria in the Municipal Code, and wind is set by the Colorado Front Range Gust Map. We size beams, posts, and footings to the numbers the reviewer will check — the difference between a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) that carries a winter and one that doesn't.

## The wildfire code is now the baseline

Black Hawk adopted the 2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code on April 1, 2026, effective July 1, 2026. Decks and exterior materials have to meet its ignition-resistance provisions, so [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) or mineral-based boards are the code-aligned way to build, with framing bays closed or screened against embers and nothing stored underneath. We were building fire-aware in the mountains before the code required it; here the code and the forest point the same direction.

## Steep sites, real structure

On Chase Gulch and Dory Hill the lots drop away fast, and an elevated walkout is often the best square footage on the property — with the real cost in the posts and footings below. We design from the actual grade, and choose slim aluminum or cable railing that keeps the trees and the gulch in view rather than caging the deck.

## Repair versus replace, at altitude

Older decks here were built to snow, frost, and hardware assumptions a SAFEbuilt reviewer would not accept today. We inspect the frame and answer plainly: sound structure earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in ignition-resistant composite; aged-out structure gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) plan on current-code framing. The answer comes in writing either way.

## Sixty-five minutes, scheduled in blocks

From our shop in Centennial, Black Hawk is about sixty-five minutes up I-70 and US-6 through Clear Creek Canyon — past our [Golden](/locations/golden) work and a few minutes short of [Central City](/locations/central-city). We schedule mountain builds in blocks rather than day trips, which keeps the drive out of your price. The [guarantee](/warranty) is the same as it is in the metro.
