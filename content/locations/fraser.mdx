## The valley floor, and the cold that comes with it

Fraser sits at 8,574 feet on the floor of the Fraser Valley, a few miles down US 40 from [Winter Park](/locations/winter-park). About 1,400 people live here year-round, and the town calls itself the Icebox of the Nation for good reason — it is famous for record winter lows. The housing splits by era: older ranch-style homes and cabins downtown around Eisenhower Drive, fast-growing townhome and single-family neighborhoods in Grand Park and the Fraser side of Rendezvous, newer homes in Ptarmigan, Fraser Valley Highlands, and Byers Peak Ranch, and workforce housing filling in between. Lots are flatter than Winter Park's, which simplifies the structure. The cold and the drifting snow do not simplify anything.

## Permits: one division for two towns

The Town of Fraser's building permits, plan review, and inspections are administered by the Town of Winter Park through the combined Winter Park–Fraser Building Division, reached at 970-726-8081. Applications are filed through Winter Park's Accela portal, and decks and accessory structures require a permit. Zoning and setback questions go to Fraser Town Hall at 153 Fraser Avenue. Grand Park and Rendezvous are master-planned communities with design-review covenants on top of the town permit, so the review committee sees the drawings first. Fraser adopted the 2021 I-Codes and the 2023 NEC, and the town has adopted the 2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code as well. We sort out which counter and which committee, prepare one clean packet, and manage inspections through final.

## Sixty psf, thirty inches, and drifting snow

Because Fraser follows the Winter Park amendments, the design numbers are the same as up the hill: uncovered decks and balconies must be designed for a 60 psf live load or the design snow load for the site, whichever is greater; ground snow loads come from the SEAC Colorado report; the frost line is 30 inches; and design wind is 105 mph. What differs is the exposure. Fraser's open valley floor lets snow drift, so it piles against the house side of the deck and stays there, and the deep cold cycles every fastener and connection through freeze and thaw all winter. We size beams, posts, and footings to the numbers, and detail ledgers, flashing, and hardware for the ice. A [covered section](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) here is engineered to carry snow, not picked from a catalog, because at this elevation the number is the design.

## Composite, for reasons the Icebox supplies

Altitude UV in summer, months under snowpack, and the hardest freeze-thaw cycling of any town we serve: this is what [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) was built for, and why we lead with it over softwood that starts an annual refinishing clock at this elevation. The wildfire code the town adopted points the same direction, favoring ignition-resistant surfaces. Where the Byers Peak view is the point, slim aluminum or cable railing keeps it.

## Repair or replace

Downtown cabins and older ranch homes often carry decks framed to lighter assumptions than the current 60 psf minimum. We inspect the frame before quoting. Sound structure gets a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite for meaningfully less than a teardown; undersized structure gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation on current-code framing — in writing either way.

Fraser is about 110 minutes from our Centennial shop over Berthoud Pass — well beyond our [Georgetown](/locations/georgetown) work. We schedule the Fraser Valley in blocks and by season — grouping site visits, deliveries, and build weeks so a crew is here for a stretch — and the pass decides winter access.
