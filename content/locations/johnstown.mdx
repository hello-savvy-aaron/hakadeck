  - q: "Do I need a permit to build a deck in Johnstown?"

  - q: "What should I budget for a deck in Johnstown?"
    a: "Capped composite installs at roughly $40–$70 per square foot, so a typical 300–400 square foot deck runs about $15,000–$30,000 including railing and stairs. A pergola or engineered cover adds to that depending on span and exposure. Our online calculator will put a planning range in front of you in half a minute."
  - q: "Does Thompson River Ranch or my subdivision need to approve the design first?"
    a: "Usually, yes. Thompson River Ranch runs its own metro district, and most of Johnstown's newer neighborhoods — Pioneer Ridge, Thompson Crossing, Johnstown Village — carry HOA or district architectural review on top of the town permit. That approval typically has to land before the permit does, and it often takes longer. We prepare both submittals and sequence them so the project keeps moving."
  - q: "Our builder deck is only a few years old but feels flimsy. Repair or replace?"
    a: "We inspect before we answer, because both happen here. Builder-minimum decks in new subdivisions are usually structurally legal but small, springy, and surfaced in the cheapest material that passed. Sometimes the frame is sound and a composite re-deck is the honest quote; sometimes the footings and framing were sized to the minimum and starting over is the better money. Either way you get a straight answer, not the more expensive one by default."

## The crossroads town that grew

Johnstown sits where I-25 meets Highway 34 — Loveland one direction, Greeley the other, Denver an hour south — and that position turned a small farm town into one of the fastest-growing addresses in Northern Colorado. The pattern is a preserved historic core downtown, ringed by post-2000 master-planned subdivisions: Thompson River Ranch, Pioneer Ridge, Thompson Crossing, Johnstown Village.

Which means most of the houses here are young, most of the yards are open, and most of the decks are whatever the production builder shipped. That's the project we do most in Johnstown: turning a builder-basic backyard into the room the house was missing.

## Starting from almost nothing is the good version

A new-build backyard — bare turf, a slab stoop, maybe a ten-by-ten platform — sounds like a disadvantage. It isn't. There's no failing structure to demolish and no old compromises to inherit. We design the [composite deck](/services/composite-decks), the shade, and the [railing](/services/railings) as one plan, size the footings correctly from day one, and the result reads like it came with the house.

If your builder deck already exists, we look before we quote. A sound frame can sometimes carry a new composite surface — a [repair-scale project](/services/deck-repair) — while minimum-spec framing is usually better replaced outright with a [deck sized for the yard](/services/deck-replacement).

## Open plains, real wind, real hail

At about 4,850 feet on open Weld County plains, Johnstown lots take wind with nothing to slow it down and sit squarely in Colorado's hail alley. Both drive how we build. A [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) on exposed ground is loaded upward in a windstorm — uplift — so engineered post bases and exposure-rated hardware are what keep it attached. And capped composite is the surface that takes hail and high-altitude UV without checking, splintering, or fading the way softwood does. Our [composite vs. hardwood guide](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) covers the trade-offs honestly.

## The clay underneath

Weld County soils run to expansive clay — ground that swells wet and shrinks dry, and heaves shallow footings out of level a season at a time. Add Front Range freeze-thaw and the answer is non-negotiable: caisson footings drilled to full frost depth, 30 to 36 inches, on every build. Nobody sees that work at the reveal. Everybody sees it ten years later.

## Permits, portals, and district review

The Town of Johnstown requires deck permits — it maintains a dedicated deck permit type — filed through the eTRAKiT portal, with SAFEbuilt handling inspections. In the master-planned neighborhoods, metro-district or HOA design review comes first and often takes longer than the permit itself. We prepare both packets, file them in the right order, and walk the project through every inspection. Our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) explains the general process if you want the background.
