## Deck installation in Aurora

If you're comparing quotes for deck installation in Aurora, here's what a correct install involves — the decking you'll walk on is the last and easiest part.

It starts underground: Colorado's frost line sits 30 to 36 inches down, and every post needs an engineered footing that reaches below it, or it heaves with the freeze-thaw cycle and takes the deck's level — and its railing lines and stair landings — with it. Footing depth and diameter go on the drawings the City of Aurora reviews before we dig; they're engineering decisions, not guesses in the hole.

Framing comes next: the ledger flashed and bolted to the house, joists sized to the tighter span composite demands, and solid blocking wherever posts and stairs load it. Then the [composite installation](/services/composite-decks) itself — hidden fasteners, correct gapping so boards can move with Aurora's temperature swings, and picture-frame borders where called for. Most Aurora decks run one to three weeks on site once the permit clears; design and city review happen before the crew arrives, so the disruption is measured in days, not months.

Not every Aurora deck needs installing from scratch — plenty of older decks just need a [repair or re-deck](/services/deck-repair). One symptom worth checking first: boards curling up at the edges. Our guide to [why deck boards cup](/blog/deck-boards-cupping) explains which cases a fastener fix solves, and which mean the surface is done — even with sound framing underneath.

## Hail alley's material problem

Southeast Aurora sits squarely in one of the most hail-prone corridors in the country — those storms are brutal on stained and painted wood decks. It's the single strongest argument for [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) in this part of the metro: the polymer cap takes hail strikes without the pitting and finish damage that send wood decks into an annual sand-and-restain cycle. Pair it with a [pergola or solid-roof cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) and the deck stays usable when the weather turns — and out here on the high plains, it turns often.

## Two generations of decks, one fix

Aurora's southeast filled in across two waves — the 1990s–2000s neighborhoods are now replacing their original builder-grade decks, while newer communities are upgrading platforms too small for how the houses live. Both projects end the same way: an engineered, permitted deck sized for real use.
