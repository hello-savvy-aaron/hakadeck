## Home ground

Arapahoe County is where Haka Decks lives — our shop is in Centennial, and most of our crew drives less than half an hour to work each morning. The county stretches from the old streetcar suburbs of Englewood and Littleton through the big 1970s-to-90s neighborhoods of Centennial and south Aurora, with the horse properties of Cherry Hills Village and the office parks of Greenwood Village in between. It is established, treed, and largely built out — which means the deck work here is less about new construction and more about what happens when an entire generation of wood decks gets old at the same time.

## Where we build in Arapahoe County

[Centennial](/locations/centennial) is home — the shop is here, and so is more of our work than anywhere else. [Littleton](/locations/littleton) brings older housing stock and some of the county's best-loved backyards; [Englewood](/locations/englewood) is compact lots where a well-designed deck earns its footprint twice over. [Greenwood Village](/locations/greenwood-village) and [Cherry Hills Village](/locations/cherry-hills-village) run to larger properties where the structure and the railing detail carry the project, and [Aurora](/locations/aurora) — the county's biggest city — spans everything from 1970s ranches to brand-new subdivisions at the eastern edge.

## Permits: mostly city counters

Unincorporated Arapahoe County addresses file with the county, but most homes here sit inside a city, and Centennial, Littleton, Englewood, Greenwood Village, Cherry Hills Village, and Aurora each run their own building departments. Typical metro review runs one to three weeks with fees in the $150–$500 range, varying by counter. None of that lands on you: we confirm the jurisdiction for your address, prepare the drawings and packet, and manage inspections through sign-off. The [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) explains the process if you like knowing how it works.

## Replacement country

Walk any street in Centennial or south Aurora and you can date the houses by the decks: pressure-treated platforms from the 70s, 80s, and 90s, most of them past the age where staining helps. This is the most common project we quote in the county — pulling an aged-out wood deck and building an engineered [composite deck](/services/composite-decks) that ends the refinishing cycle for good. Where the frame is still sound, a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) saves real money; where it is not, we say so and price a proper [replacement](/services/deck-replacement). If you are not sure which side of that line your deck is on, our [deck safety inspection guide](/deck-safety-inspection-guide) is a fair place to start, and the [repair cost guide](/blog/deck-repair-cost-denver) has honest numbers.

## Shade, structure, and the long game

The county's mature trees help, but plenty of these backyards still bake, and a [pergola or covered section](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) is the difference between a deck used in June and one used all year. On walkout and view lots, the [railing](/services/railings) decision usually matters more to the finished result than the deck boards do. Everything we build carries the same [guarantee](/warranty), and our [process](/process) is the same whether the job is three streets from the shop or across the county.

## Twenty minutes to most of it

From Centennial, nearly all of Arapahoe County is inside a twenty-minute drive — which means fast site visits, easy mid-build presence, and scheduling that flexes around you.
