## The wind is the headline

Most Colorado towns make you design for snow. Estes Park makes you design for wind first. The town sits in a designated ASCE 7 special wind region for Front Range downslope windstorms — the Chinook events that pour off the Divide — and Larimer County requires design wind speeds taken from the SEAC Front Range Gust Map, with values from 115 mph up to 225 mph depending on the site.

That has a practical consequence most homeowners don't see coming: prescriptive deck design under the IRC is only permitted below 140 mph ultimate wind and 70 psf ground snow. Many Estes lots map above that line, which means an engineered deck — calculated connections, uplift hardware, footings sized for the site — is a code requirement, not an upsell. We build that engineering in from the first drawing.

## Snow, altitude, and the surface that survives both

At 7,522 feet, the county's minimum design roof snow load of 35 psf governs every [covered structure](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), and the framing under an open deck gets sized for real mountain snow, not metro snow. The surface takes the other half of the punishment: the hardest UV we build under anywhere, deep freeze-thaw cycling, and long snow cover. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) handles that without the yearly refinishing a wood deck demands at this altitude — our [materials comparison](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) is candid about the trade-offs.

One consideration unique to this town: the resident elk herd. Elk lean on, rub against, and walk through landscaping and railings, so we spec [railing systems](/services/railings) with that abuse in mind — sturdy posts, no fragile infill at ground level — while keeping the sightlines that steep view lots exist for.

## Cabins, customs, and rental math

Estes housing runs from mid-century cabins in neighborhoods like Carriage Hills to custom mountain homes in Fall River Estates and Stanley Heights, with a heavy short-term-rental presence throughout. For rental owners, the deck is often the listing photo — and a low-maintenance surface matters when the property earns between cleanings, not weekends you spend staining.

For the older cabin stock, we inspect before quoting: sound framing earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) for far less than a teardown, while frames that predate today's wind and snow criteria get an honest [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation. Our [inspection guide](/deck-safety-inspection-guide) covers what we look for.

## Fire is part of the design brief

The full-town evacuation during the October 2020 East Troublesome Fire settled any debate about whether Estes Park is wildland-urban interface. On these lots we default to ignition-resistant surfaces, screened framing bays so embers can't nest under the deck, and an underside that isn't storage. It doesn't make a deck fireproof; it makes it a smaller liability on the worst day.

## Permits in town and in the valley

Inside town limits, the Town of Estes Park's Division of Building Safety issues the permit; unincorporated Estes Valley properties go through Larimer County, and electrical permits run through the state. We handle the drawings, the engineering package, the filing, and inspections through sign-off.
