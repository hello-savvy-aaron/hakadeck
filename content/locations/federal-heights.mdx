## A compact city on the north side

Federal Heights is a small Adams County city of about 14,000 people at roughly 5,400 feet, packed along the Federal Boulevard and 92nd Avenue corridors near Water World and Hyland Hills. The housing is compact and flat: 1960s–80s ranches and split-levels on small lots, plus a high share of manufactured-home communities. There is not much sloped ground or acreage — small footprints, close neighbors, and plenty of original decks near the end of their service life.

## The city's own counter, four days a week

Federal Heights issues its own permits through the City of Federal Heights Building Department at 2380 W. 90th Ave., which is a separate counter from Adams County, Westminster, and Thornton. City offices run a four-day week, Tuesday through Friday, 7:00am to 5:30pm, and inspections are scheduled through a dedicated line, so we plan build weeks around that calendar rather than losing a Monday to a locked door. Applications go through the city's online permit portal; we prepare the drawings, file, and carry the permit through final. Background in our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) and on the [Adams County](/locations/adams-county) page.

## Flat lots, open sky

Flat ground keeps the structure simple — no stepped footings, no tall posts — but the north metro is open country, and wind, hail, and high-altitude UV are what age a deck here. Footings go below the 30–36 inch frost line, and any [covered section or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) gets connections sized for uplift, since a roof on an open lot wants to leave in a spring gust. Hail is why we lead with [capped composite](/services/composite-decks): it won't check, splinter, or fade like softwood, and it needs a rinse instead of a stain. We install Trex, Deckorators, and TimberTech as a certified dealer.

## Small footprints, done well

Most Federal Heights decks are modest — a landing and a platform off the back door, or a split-level's mid-height deck over a walkout patio. Small does not mean simple: the stairs, the railing, and the ledger flashing are where inexpensive decks fail, and they are where we spend the care. On a manufactured home, the deck is either freestanding or attached to a proper structural connection, never hung off siding.

## Repair or replace

The 1960s–80s ranches and split-levels here carry a lot of original or second-generation wood decks, and the question we get most is whether the frame can be saved. We inspect before quoting. Sound joists, posts, and a bolted ledger earn a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite at $20–$35 per square foot; rot at grade, a nailed ledger, or footings above frost get a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation, in writing.
