## The county seat on the edge of the plains

Kiowa is a home-rule town of about 750 people at 6,388 feet, where the pines of the Palmer Divide give out and the eastern plains begin. It is the Elbert County seat, which is why a town this small has both a town hall and the county building department on the same street. The housing is older frame homes and ranch houses on in-town lots along Comanche Street and in Fawn Valley, with open-prairie acreage in every direction — up the Kiowa Creek corridor, out CO-86 to the east, around the fairgrounds. Many Kiowa addresses are well outside town limits, and that is where the permit question starts.

## Two counters, two blocks apart

Inside town limits, the Town of Kiowa Building Department issues the permit. Plan review and inspections run through SAFEbuilt, the town's contracted reviewer, under the 2018 IBC, and applications go through the Community Core portal. A structure over 120 square feet typically needs a permit, and no contractor license is required to pull a town one.

Outside town limits, the Elbert County Building Department at 215 Comanche Street — two blocks from Town Hall — issues the permit instead, and the county does require licensed contractors. Same street, different rulebook. We confirm which side of the line your parcel is on before drawing anything, then prepare the packet, file it, and run inspections through sign-off. The [Elbert County hub](/locations/elbert-county) lays out the county-versus-town split for the whole county, and our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the general process.

## Thirty-five pounds of snow and 110 miles an hour of wind

Kiowa's town code sets ground snow load at 35 psf and the design wind speed at 110 mph, and both numbers are earned. The Divide catches heavy, wet upslope snow, and the exposed plains site means storms arrive with nothing in the way. The snow figure sizes the beams and posts under a [covered deck or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers); the wind figure sizes the connections, because a roofed structure out here is loaded upward in a storm and engineered post bases and hardware are what keep it on the posts. Frost depth along the Front Range runs 30–36 inches, which sets the footings.

Hail comes with the territory too. It is a large part of why we lead with [capped composite](/services/composite-decks): it takes hail, altitude UV, and freeze-thaw without the annual refinishing that wood demands out here. Where cedar or hardwood still makes sense, we say so.

## Decks sized for acreage

A prairie lot changes the brief. Decks here tend to be larger and lower, wrapping the house and connecting to the yard rather than perching over it, and a covered section is often what makes the space usable in wind. Ranch owners usually want the sightline to the barn and the horizon kept open, which slim aluminum or cable [railing](/services/railings) handles far better than bulky pickets. The covered deck cost guide has honest numbers for the roofed portion.

## Older decks, honest answers

Kiowa's older frame homes often carry decks built to softer lumber and lighter hardware than current code. We inspect the frame first: sound structure earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite for much less than a teardown, while framing that is genuinely done gets a straight [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation, in writing either way. The inspection is free, and so is the verdict.
