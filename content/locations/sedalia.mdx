## Where the county starts to climb

Sedalia is a small unincorporated community at about 5,840 feet, sitting where Highway 67 leaves the Plum Creek valley and heads up Jarre Canyon toward the Pike National Forest. The townsite itself is a few hundred people. The housing that surrounds it is what brings us here: rural acreage and ranchettes along Highway 67 and Perry Park Road, a mix of older farmhouses, 1970s–90s custom homes, and newer foothills builds on sloped, pine-covered lots that climb toward the Divide.

That climb is the design brief. A deck on a valley-floor parcel and a deck a few miles up Perry Park Road are engineered to different numbers, and we build to the number for your lot.

## One permit counter: Douglas County

There is no town hall in Sedalia and no municipal building department. The Douglas County Building Division in Castle Rock issues every deck permit here, the same counter we file with for [Franktown](/locations/franktown) and Roxborough Park and most [Larkspur](/locations/larkspur) addresses. The county publishes a Deck Submittal Requirements Checklist and a Residential Deck Detail, and it makes two things clear up front: where a covenant applies, get HOA or architectural committee approval before applying, and the owner must verify utility and easement locations before excavation. On acreage that can mean a well line or septic field, which is exactly the kind of thing to know before a footing auger shows up.

We confirm your parcel, prepare one clean packet, and run inspections through final. Background in our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro), and the county hub for everything else at [Douglas County](/locations/douglas-county).

## Snow by the foot, footings by the frost

Douglas County tiers design snow load by elevation, and Sedalia spans three tiers. The townsite sits in the 30 psf band (5,152–5,999 feet); lots between 6,000 and 6,499 feet carry 35 psf; and above 6,500 feet the figure is 40 psf. Ground load equals roof load, with no reductions. That number sizes every beam and post under a [covered deck or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), so we check the grade elevation on the parcel before drawing anything. Frost depth is a 36-inch minimum countywide, which sets the footings. An older deck framed to the wrong tier is the most common thing we find on inspection.

## Built for acreage and slope

Acreage decks are bigger, lower, and more connected to the land than suburban ones: wraparounds, covered sections that stretch the season, railing chosen to keep the pines and the canyon in view rather than fence them off. On the sloped lots up toward the forest, the structure below the deck is where the budget goes — taller posts, stepped footings, and an underside we keep clear of stored fuel.

We lead with [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) because this climate is what it was built for: altitude UV, hard freeze-thaw cycling, heavy spring snow, summer hail. Wood at this elevation starts an annual refinishing clock. Where cedar or hardwood still makes sense, we say so.

## Older decks, straight answers

Sedalia's housing spans decades, and many older decks were framed before the current snow tiers and hardware standards. We inspect the frame before quoting. Sound structure earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite for a fraction of a rebuild; an undersized or failing frame gets a replacement recommendation on current-code structure. The inspection is free, and so is the verdict.
