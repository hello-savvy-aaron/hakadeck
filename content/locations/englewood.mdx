## The close-in suburb, with close-in constraints

Englewood is one of the metro's original streetcar-era suburbs, and its housing shows it: compact lots, a lot of post-war brick ranches and bungalows, detached garages off alleys, and neighbors close enough to wave at from the back step. That geometry changes what a good deck looks like. Sprawling multi-level layouts that suit a Parker walk-out would swallow an Englewood backyard whole. What works here is precision — a deck sized so there's still yard left, built-in seating that saves the square footage furniture would eat, and privacy handled by design rather than by fence height.

The city's map names 40 neighborhoods — Hampden Hills, Cherrelyn, Jackson Heights, Broadway Heights, Centennial Acres — and the small-lot rule holds across most of them. Arapahoe Acres is the exception: a National Register district of International- and Usonian-style modern homes built 1949–1957 between Bates and Dartmouth, where a deck has to sit low and not fight the house.

## Our mail says Englewood too

Postal Englewood is much bigger than the City of Englewood. The 80112 and 80111 ZIPs run east across the Denver Tech Center, so a great many homes and businesses with an Englewood address sit in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), [Greenwood Village](/locations/greenwood-village), or unincorporated Arapahoe County for permitting purposes. Our own shop off Easter Lane is a textbook case — Englewood mail, Centennial jurisdiction.

It matters because those counters have different submittal requirements, different review timelines, and no shared paperwork. We confirm whose jurisdiction your address is actually in, produce the drawings to that counter's standard, pull the [permit](/blog/deck-permits-centennial-arapahoe-douglas), and manage the inspections. You never have to work out which city you technically live in. We've lived it ourselves: before Easter Lane, Haka ran out of South Geneva Circle, another address that says Englewood on the envelope.

## What the City of Englewood asks for on a deck permit

Inside the city limits, the permit runs through the Building and Codes Division at 1000 Englewood Parkway, filed online. The only decks that skip it are detached, under 200 square feet, not serving an exit door, and no more than 30 inches above grade — and even those need zoning sign-off for setbacks. Anything attached to the house needs the full permit.

Englewood builds to the 2024 International Residential Code with a 36-inch frost depth and a 30-pound ground snow load. The city's posted deck guide wants a scaled site plan with distances to property lines, 36-inch guards on any deck more than 30 inches up, and lateral-load connections at the ledger. Contractors also need a city license. Our [south-metro permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the neighboring counters.

## Shade, privacy, and west-facing afternoons

The two requests we hear most in Englewood are shade and privacy, and they're usually the same problem. Lots run tight, west exposures bake from mid-afternoon on, and a bare patio slab goes unused all summer for both reasons at once. A [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) solves the sun; slat screens and thoughtful [railing](/services/railings) choices — cable or aluminum instead of a solid wall — buy privacy without turning the yard into a box.

## Repairs, replacements, and honest answers

A lot of Englewood's decks are older than the homeowners who inherited them, and by Colorado standards that means checking the structure before anything else. We inspect the ledger, footings, and framing the way an engineer would and tell you straight whether you're looking at a [repair](/services/deck-repair), a re-deck over sound framing, or a full [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) in [capped composite](/services/composite-decks). Sometimes the answer saves you money; either way you get it in writing, and everything we build is covered by our [written guarantee](/warranty).

On a first-generation wood deck we check three things first: a ledger nailed rather than bolted, footings short of the city's 36-inch frost line, and a guard under today's 36-inch minimum. The ledger is the serious one — a [deck pulling away from the house](/blog/deck-pulling-away-from-house) is structural, not cosmetic. Re-decking over sound framing runs about $20–$35 per square foot; our [deck repair cost guide](/blog/deck-repair-cost-denver) and the [calculator](/deck-cost-calculator) put numbers on both paths.
