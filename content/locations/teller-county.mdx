## The high county

Teller County is the highest ground we build on. It starts where Ute Pass tops out at Woodland Park and runs west and south across 8,000 to 9,500 feet of pine and aspen — Divide, Florissant, and the mining towns of Cripple Creek and Victor at the far end. The housing is cabins, log homes, and 1970s–2010s custom builds on wooded 2–35 acre parcels, and the ground under them is rocky. Long winters and deep snow shape every deck here, and so does the parcel: sloped, shaded, and often a long way from the nearest flat spot.

## Where we build in Teller County

[Woodland Park](/locations/woodland-park) is the county's biggest town and the heart of our Teller County work — 1970s–2000s mountain homes on pine lots with Pikes Peak views, plus newer subdivisions. [Green Mountain Falls](/locations/green-mountain-falls) straddles the county line with [El Paso County](/locations/el-paso-county) in a shaded valley just below the pass. West of Woodland Park, the Divide and Florissant country is acreage: cabins and custom homes on 2–35 acre parcels where the deck is often the best room on the property. Cripple Creek and Victor are the far end; ask, and the answer is usually yes with a longer lead. Coming up from the Springs, our Cascade work sits at the bottom of the same pass.

## Permits: the county counter, and Woodland Park's two steps

The Teller County Building Division, at 800 Research Drive in Woodland Park, issues permits for unincorporated parcels — Divide, Florissant, and the acreage around them. It publishes a Residential Deck Permit Application and Minor Deck Site Plan Requirements, and the county code adopts the 2021 IBC and IRC, 2018 IECC, and 2023 NEC with local amendments. Inside the City of Woodland Park the process is different: the city issues a Zoning Development Permit for setback review first, then the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department issues the building permit — PPRBD serves the city and no other part of the county. Green Mountain Falls permits through PPRBD for the whole town, county line or not. Cripple Creek and Victor have their own arrangements. We confirm the right counter for your parcel, prepare one clean packet, and manage inspections through final.

## Fifty-seven psf, thirty inches of frost

The 2023 Teller County Building Code sets ground snow load at 57 psf and roof snow load at 40 psf for all structures, with no reductions, and a minimum frost depth of 30 inches. Woodland Park, under PPRBD, uses the same 57/40 figures. Those numbers size every beam, post, and footing under a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), and at this elevation they describe an ordinary winter: snow that lands in November stays until spring. Older cabin decks framed to lighter assumptions deserve a look before the next big storm.

## Cabins, acreage, and composite

The most common Teller County project is replacing a cabin-era wood deck that has spent decades under snow, shade, and altitude UV. Where the frame is sound we say so and price a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) at $20–$35 per square foot; where it is undersized for the snow tier or the footings never reached frost, a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) built once beats repairing twice, and we put that in writing. Composite earns its place up here because it handles months of ice and thaw with soap and water. On acreage with a view, a slim aluminum or cable railing keeps it.

## Ninety-five minutes and up, in blocks

From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), Woodland Park is about ninety-five minutes and the Divide and Florissant country is further. We are plain about it: mountain work is scheduled in blocks, with batched site visits and build days run back to back rather than one-off trips. Price and the warranty do not change with the mile marker; lead time does, and the short season rewards planning early.
