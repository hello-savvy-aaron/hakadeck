## A few hundred homes along Bear Creek

Idledale is the first canyon community you reach heading up Highway 74 out of Morrison — a few hundred homes strung along Bear Creek next to Lair o' the Bear Park, at about 6,600 feet. The housing is a mix of 1920s cabins, 1970s A-frames, and newer custom homes, almost all on steep, shaded canyon lots, with Grapevine Road climbing out of the canyon above the townsite. Building envelopes are small and the grade is real. A deck here is often the only flat outdoor space on the property, and it is usually standing on posts well above the ground.

## Permits: Jefferson County Building Safety

Idledale has no town hall. It is unincorporated Jefferson County, so Jeffco Building Safety in Golden issues the deck permit, with applications and inspection requests running through the county's CitizenPortal. Since July 1, 2026, the county's Wildfire Resiliency Code applies to permits in unincorporated Jeffco: on parcels inside the mapped WUI overlay, deck surfaces must be noncombustible and framing must limit ignition, and that applies to exterior alterations such as deck replacements and additions. Map status is parcel-specific — Class 1, Class 2, or outside the overlay — and we check your address with the county before designing. Our permit guide covers the process, and our [Jefferson County](/locations/jefferson-county) page covers the county.

## Snow at the first elevation break

Jefferson County tiers snow load by elevation. The table starts at 43 psf ground snow below 6,500 feet and climbs to 54 psf at 7,000, so Idledale parcels land around 43–46 psf — more than a Denver deck, less than [Evergreen](/locations/evergreen) or Conifer up the hill. That number sizes every beam and post under a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers). Frost depth is 36 inches, wind is designed at 129 mph in Exposure B, and weathering is classed severe. Canyon shade adds its own factor: snow that would melt off a sunny metro deck in a day sits here for weeks, so we spec freeze-thaw-rated connectors and a surface that does not mind staying wet.

## Building on the grade

Steep lots mean stepped footings, taller posts, and decks that cantilever over the slope honestly rather than pretending the ground is flat. The structure below the deck is where the budget's center of gravity sits, and it is where we spend the engineering. Up top, [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) is the code-aligned surface on WUI-mapped parcels and the practical choice everywhere in the canyon, since it skips the refinishing clock that shade and altitude UV start on softwood. Slim aluminum or cable railing keeps the creek and the trees in view. Our composite vs. hardwood comparison is honest about the trade-offs if you are weighing real wood.

## Cabins and A-frames: repair or replace

Plenty of Idledale decks date to the cabin and A-frame eras, built to snow loads and hardware standards the county no longer accepts. We inspect before we quote. Sound framing gets a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) at $20–$35 per square foot over the existing structure; aged-out framing gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) on current-code structure, which on mapped parcels must carry a noncombustible surface anyway. The answer comes in writing either way, and the deck safety inspection guide shows what we look at.
