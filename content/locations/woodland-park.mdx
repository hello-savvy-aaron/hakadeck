## The City Above the Clouds

Woodland Park sits at 8,465 feet on the far side of Ute Pass, the biggest town in [Teller County](/locations/teller-county) and the highest place we build. The housing is mostly 1970s–2000s mountain homes on pine-covered lots — Paradise Estates, Sunnywood, Morning Sun, Trail Ridge, and the Rampart Range Road side of town — plus newer subdivisions, and nearly all of it has some version of the Pikes Peak view. Two things shape every deck here: a snow season that runs roughly half the year, and elk that treat yards as a thoroughfare. We design for both.

## Permits: the city first, then PPRBD

Woodland Park runs a two-step process. Step one is the City's Planning and Building Department at 220 W South Avenue, which issues a Multi-Use Zoning Development Permit — the setback review. Step two is the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department, which takes the construction drawings after city sign-off, issues the building permit, and runs inspections, the same counter that serves Colorado Springs and [Green Mountain Falls](/locations/green-mountain-falls). PPRBD serves the City of Woodland Park and no other part of Teller County, so a Woodland Park address outside city limits files with the Teller County Building Division instead. We confirm which applies to your parcel, prepare one packet, and carry it through final.

## Fifty-seven psf, and a long winter

Above the 7,000-foot line, PPRBD's design criteria step up to 57 psf ground snow and 40 psf flat-roof snow; Teller County's own code uses the same figures with a 30-inch minimum frost depth. That number sizes every beam, post, and footing under a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), and at 8,465 feet it is not conservative — it is the winter. Snow that lands in November is still there in April. An older uncovered deck framed to a lighter assumption is worth checking before the next big storm. A covered section, built to the load, is what turns a Woodland Park deck from a four-month room into a year-round one.

## Composite, at 8,465 feet

Altitude UV, hail, months of ice, and daily freeze-thaw on the shoulders of the season: this is the weather that wrecks wood finishes in a couple of summers. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) handles all of it with soap and water, which is why we lead with it here. And because the view is the point on most of these lots, a slim aluminum or cable railing keeps Pikes Peak in the frame.

## Repair or replace

Many Woodland Park decks date to the 1970s–1990s, built before the current snow tier and hardware standards. We inspect the frame before quoting. Sound structure earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite at $20–$35 per square foot, well under a teardown; an undersized frame or shallow footings get a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation in writing. We photograph what we find and explain the call.

## Ninety-five minutes, in blocks

From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), Woodland Park is about ninety-five minutes — I-25 south, US-24 up through Ute Pass past [Cascade](/locations/cascade). We are plain about the distance: mountain work is scheduled in blocks, with batched site visits and build days run back to back rather than one-off trips. Price and the warranty do not change with the mile marker; lead time does, and the short season rewards planning early — fall contracts get the first spring build slots.
