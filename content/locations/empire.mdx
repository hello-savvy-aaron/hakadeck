## A silver camp on the way to Berthoud Pass

Empire is small — about 345 people — and old, an 1860s silver camp in a sunny, star-shaped valley where US 40 leaves the I-70 corridor and starts climbing toward Berthoud Pass and Winter Park. The Park Avenue historic core is compact lots with small frame homes and cabins from the mining era, with newer houses scattered up North Empire Road and along Bard Creek. The Original Hard Rock Cafe has been serving here since 1934. At 8,615 feet, the town sits high enough that snow is the design brief for anything built outdoors.

## Permits: we confirm the path before we draw

Here is what the research shows, plainly. The Town of Empire publishes no building-permit application — its Applications & Permits page posts excavation and undergrounding, conditional-use, and short-term-rental forms, nothing for a deck. Town zoning runs under Ordinance 170, with the Planning Commission reviewing land-use items, and the Town Clerk at Town Hall on East Park Avenue is the first call for any permit question. The Clear Creek County Building Department in Georgetown serves unincorporated parcels and answers code questions, with applications through its GovBuilt portal. So before we design anything in Empire, we call Town Hall and confirm whether the town or the county processes a residential deck permit for your address, then prepare one clean packet for whichever counter it is. The [Clear Creek County](/locations/clear-creek-county) page covers the county side in more detail.

## Snow by elevation, piers at 36 inches

Empire does not publish its own design loads, so the county's design criteria are the reference: a 42-inch minimum frost line, deck piers at least 36 inches below grade, and a roof snow load looked up by elevation on the county's ClearMap table rather than a single figure. At roughly 8,600 feet on the pass approach, expect the heavy end of that table, plus drifting where the valley funnels wind. That is what sizes every beam, post, and footing under a [covered deck](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), and it is why we engineer to the parcel instead of a generic mountain detail. The county also requires 2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code compliance and a three-zone defensible-space plan with county permits; we confirm with Town Hall whether that reaches your lot, and we detail for embers regardless.

## Composite on compact lots

An Empire deck is usually modest in footprint and hard-working: a cabin's only outdoor room, shoveled all winter and baked by altitude sun all summer. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) handles both — no annual refinishing, no meltwater soaking into the boards before a freeze — and it is what we lead with here. Slim aluminum or cable [railing](/services/railings) keeps the valley walls in view instead of behind pickets, and on a small lot that view is most of the reason for the deck.

## Old cabin decks, inspected first

Many Empire decks were added to mining-era houses long ago, framed light and hung on unflashed ledgers. We look before we quote. Sound framing earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite for far less than a teardown; an undersized or rotted frame gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation on current-code structure. The answer comes in writing either way.

## A little over an hour, scheduled in blocks

Empire is about 65 minutes from our Centennial shop, up I-70 past [Idaho Springs](/locations/idaho-springs) and onto US 40, with [Georgetown](/locations/georgetown) a few miles further along the interstate. We group mountain-corridor work so a crew stays on site for consecutive days rather than commuting for half-days; the drive shapes the calendar, not the price or the guarantee behind the work.
