## The highest county we build in

Summit County is Colorado's highest-population ski county, and almost none of it sits below 8,800 feet. The housing splits three ways: resort condos at Keystone and Copper Mountain, 1970s-to-90s hillside subdivisions like Wildernest, Summit Cove, and Dillon Valley, and custom homes on forested lots that climb past 10,000 feet around Breckenridge and Blue River. Nearly every one has a view deck facing the Tenmile or Gore ranges, and nearly every one has to carry a snow load that would flatten a Denver-suburb build.

## Where we build in Summit County

[Breckenridge](/locations/breckenridge) is the county seat and the heaviest snow load we design for, with town code setting 90 psf below 10,000 feet and 100 psf above. [Frisco](/locations/frisco) is the walkable lakeside grid under the Tenmile Range, with Frisco Heights and Bills Ranch climbing above Main Street. [Silverthorne](/locations/silverthorne) is the year-round hub on the Blue River, with Wildernest and Mesa Cortina on its west-facing hillsides. [Dillon](/locations/dillon) sits on the reservoir with a two-step permit. Beyond the towns we serve the unincorporated county: Keystone, Copper Mountain, Blue River, Summit Cove, and the Wildernest and Mesa Cortina subdivisions above Silverthorne.

## Permits: one county counter, three town departments

The Summit County Building Inspection Department is at the County Commons on Peak One Drive in Frisco, not in the county seat, and it issues permits for all unincorporated addresses through the eTRAKiT portal. By agreement it also handles the building permit for the Town of Dillon, after Dillon's Planning Department issues a town development permit. Silverthorne and Frisco run their own building departments with plan review and inspections contracted to CBCS, and Breckenridge runs its own Building Division on Tyler EnerGov. Every county permit also needs fire-district sign-off — Summit Fire & EMS or Red, White & Blue — on its fire-hazard mitigation plan. The county adopted the 2024 I-Codes with amendments effective July 1, 2026. We confirm the counter from your parcel, prepare one packet, and manage inspections through final.

## The wildfire code sets the surface

Summit County applies the Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code to every permit applied for after May 4, 2026, and the whole county is designated moderate-to-high fire intensity, which triggers Class 2 hardening. For decks, that is specific: deck surfaces and stair treads must be noncombustible, Class A-rated, fire-retardant-treated exterior wood, or ignition-resistant material. New homes of 4,500 square feet and up need sprinklers, roofs must be Class A, vents are capped at 3/16 inch, and defensible space is required. In practice, [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) that meets the ignition-resistance standard is the surface most Summit County decks will get, and we detail the framing underneath so embers have nowhere to nest.

## Snow by subdivision, footings at forty inches

The county's Building Inspection Matrix assigns roof snow loads by subdivision rather than as one figure: Town of Dillon 70 psf, Keystone-area PUDs 75, Frisco Heights 80, Breckenridge-area subdivisions 90 to 100. Frost depth is 40 inches countywide, design wind is 115 mph, and seismic is Zone B. Those numbers size every beam and post under a covered deck or patio cover, and they are why a Breckenridge cover carries roughly twice the structure of a Dillon one. Our guide to how much snow a deck can hold explains the difference. Where an existing frame is sound we price a re-deck; where it was built to lighter assumptions we recommend a replacement, in writing either way.
