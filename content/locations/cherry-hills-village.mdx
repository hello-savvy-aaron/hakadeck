## Estate lots change the brief

Cherry Hills Village sits ten minutes north of our shop, and almost nothing about it is typical. Lots run from a third of an acre to several acres, parts of the village still carry equestrian zoning, and the housing stock is a split screen: midcentury homes being renovated or replaced, and newer custom builds in Cherry Hills Farm, Glenmoor, and Buell Mansion. On ground like that, a deck is rarely a rectangle off the kitchen door. It is one piece of a larger outdoor plan — and it gets designed, priced, and reviewed accordingly.

## Built as rooms, not a platform

The estate-scale projects we like best treat the outdoors as a set of connected rooms: a dining zone under a [pergola or solid patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), an [outdoor kitchen](/services/outdoor-kitchens) that actually gets used because it sits near the indoor one, open decking where the sun is welcome, and [railing](/services/railings) chosen to frame the yard rather than fence it off. Designing those zones together — one structure, one elevation plan, one contract — is cheaper and better-looking than adding them one summer at a time.

## Material honesty at this price point

Our default is [capped composite](/services/composite-decks): at 5,400 feet, Colorado sun degrades wood finishes fast, freeze-thaw works every fastener, and hail tests every surface. Composite takes all three better than softwood and asks almost nothing in maintenance. That said, some Cherry Hills homes genuinely call for [hardwood](/services/hardwood-cedar-decks) — ipe and its peers have a depth composite does not, and on a house where everything else is real material, we will make that case honestly, including the oiling schedule that comes with it.

## The renovation wave and the decks it exposes

A lot of Cherry Hills work starts with a renovation that makes the existing deck look — and measure — like what it is: a structure from a different era of the house. Different codes, different hardware, often a different footprint than the new plan wants. Where the framing is sound, we repair and reface. Where it is not, we say so plainly, because [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) done once beats new boards on old bones done twice.

## Permits and design review

The City of Cherry Hills Village issues its own permits through its Building Division, and decks and exterior alterations explicitly require one. The city also expects design review — drawings here need to be complete, dimensioned, and presentable, and the review culture rewards submittals that respect the neighborhood. We prepare the packet, file it, and manage inspections through final sign-off. Our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers how the process works across the south metro if you want the background.
