faqs:
  - q: "Who issues deck permits in Littleton?"
    a: "It depends on your address — postal Littleton spans the City of Littleton plus unincorporated Arapahoe, Jefferson, and Douglas County, and the separately incorporated towns of Columbine Valley and Bow Mar. None of them share paperwork. We figure out whose jurisdiction you're actually in, produce the drawings to that counter's standard, pull the permit, and manage inspections. It's a small thing to get wrong and a maddening thing to redo, so we just handle it."
  - q: "Should I replace my old Littleton deck or just re-deck it?"
    a: "We inspect first and quote honestly. Much of Littleton's housing went up from the 1960s through the 1980s, and a forty-year-old redwood deck usually needs a full replacement on code-current framing. But when the existing framing is genuinely sound, we say so and quote a re-deck instead — a new composite surface over the structure you already own, for meaningfully less."
  - q: "How much does a deck replacement cost in Littleton?"
    a: "A capped-composite rebuild follows new-build pricing plus demolition — most Denver-metro projects land in the $40–$70 per square foot installed range, with teardown and haul-off adding a modest, size-dependent line item. A re-deck over sound framing runs meaningfully less. Every quote starts with a free structural look at what you have."
  - q: "Can you design a deck around our mature trees?"
    a: "Gladly — it's what makes Littleton yards special. Neighborhoods like The Village, Southbridge, and Bow Mar South carry canopy that new-build suburbs won't match for decades, and good deck design works with it: wrapped corners, split levels that step around trunks, and shade structures that fill the gaps the trees don't cover."
  - q: "Does an easement or the South Platte floodplain affect where I can build?"
    a: "It can, and it's worth checking before you fall in love with a layout. Older Littleton properties often carry irrigation-ditch easements — the historic City Ditch among them — along with utility and drainage easements, and lots near the river can sit within mapped floodplain. All of that shapes setbacks and what a reviewer will approve. We check it against your plat and the jurisdiction's mapping during design, so it surfaces on paper rather than at the permit counter."
  - q: "Do you build curved decks?"
    a: "Yes — and we're one of the few deck contractors in the south metro who will quote one enthusiastically. Curved framing, kerfed or heat-formed composite border boards, and radius railing sections take more layout time and premium materials than a straight edge, so we price the difference honestly during the initial consultation. On the right Littleton lot — a mature tree to wrap, a sloped corner to trace — a curve is worth every dollar."
  - q: "Do you build front porches, or just backyard decks?"
    a: "Both. Littleton's older blocks are full of original front porches with sagging posts, spalled concrete, and railings that predate today's code. We rebuild them — structure, columns, decking, rails, and tongue-and-groove ceilings — with the same permits-and-inspections process as our backyard deck projects."
  - q: "When should we start if we want the deck ready for summer?"
    a: "Earlier than most people expect. Between design, HOA or town review where it applies, and a two-to-four-week permit window, plan on four to eight weeks before the crew starts — so a deck you want finished for graduation parties and the Western Welcome Week stretch in August should be in design by spring at the latest. Winter is genuinely the smartest time to sign; the paperwork runs while nobody's using the yard anyway."

## Littleton's decks are due

Littleton is one of the metro's older suburbs — much of its housing went up from the 1960s through the 1980s, and a striking number of those homes still carry their original or second wood deck. At Colorado altitude, a forty-year-old redwood deck isn't a charming original feature; it's a liability with splinters. The bulk of our Littleton work is honest replacement: tear out the tired structure, rebuild in [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) on code-current framing, and give the house an outdoor room that outlasts the roof. When the old framing is genuinely sound, we say so and quote a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) instead.

The tell is rarely dramatic. It's a slight give underfoot near the house, a railing that moves more than it should, or fasteners backing out along a south-facing edge. By the time those show up the ledger connection usually deserves a look, and that's the part of a deck that fails expensively. Our [deck replacement](/services/deck-replacement) work almost always starts with a free structural inspection and a straight answer about which of the three options — repair, re-deck, replace — you're actually looking at.

## Which Littleton are you in?

Here's the local wrinkle nobody warns you about: "Littleton" is far bigger than the City of Littleton, and your mailing address doesn't tell you who issues your permit.

Depending on where you actually sit, a Littleton deck permit may run through the City of Littleton, unincorporated **Arapahoe, Jefferson, or Douglas County**, or one of the separately incorporated towns tucked inside the postal boundary — **Columbine Valley** and **Bow Mar** both run their own town governments. Ken Caryl addresses add a substantial HOA architectural review on the Jefferson County side, and Roxborough sits in Douglas County entirely. Six possible counters, no shared paperwork, different submittal standards, different review calendars.

Two more things worth checking before a layout gets locked: older Littleton lots frequently carry irrigation-ditch easements — the historic City Ditch runs through this part of the valley — plus the usual utility and drainage easements, and properties near the South Platte can fall inside mapped floodplain. Both affect where a structure can legally go.

We sort all of it out. We confirm the jurisdiction, check your plat and the mapping during design, produce drawings to the right standard, pull the [permit](/blog/deck-permits-centennial-arapahoe-douglas), and manage the inspections wherever they run. You never stand at a counter, and you never find out in week three that you drew the deck over an easement.
