## Nine thousand feet on the Peak to Peak

Ward is an 1860s mining camp at 9,253 feet, a townsite of about 150 people just off the Peak to Peak Highway (CO 72) with Left Hand Canyon dropping away below it. The housing is old miners' cabins and self-built homes on steep, rocky lots, and Roosevelt National Forest surrounds all of it. Nothing about this ground is generic. The elevation sets the snow load, the slope sets the footings, and the forest sets the material — and we design to all three before we talk about board colors.

## The permit path, honestly

Ward's permitting runs through [Boulder County](/locations/boulder-county) Community Planning & Permitting for practical purposes; we confirm the path with the county before designing. We could not verify a Town of Ward building department, and the county's own page says it issues permits for unincorporated land and directs in-town projects to the municipality — so the first call on any Ward project is to the county office at 303-441-3930 to establish which counter reviews the deck. Once that is settled, we build the packet to the county's Residential Deck Requirements checklist, file it, and manage inspections through final sign-off. That is not a formality we skip; a permit filed at the wrong counter costs weeks.

## The county's highest snow bands

Boulder County's ground snow load map shows its highest bands, 75–100 psf, in the high country around Ward. The parcel figure is confirmed at plan review, and it sizes every beam, post, and footing. Under the county's deck rules, parcels above 70 psf need a design by a Colorado-licensed engineer or architect, so we plan on a stamped drawing from the first sketch rather than discovering the requirement at review. A [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) up here carries that load as a roof, and footings go below the 30–36 inch frost line. This is arithmetic, not instinct.

## Wildfire Zone 1, by default

The mountains around Ward are Boulder County Wildfire Zone 1-West County, where Section R390 applies to county permits: ignition-resistant decking, 2x or heavy-timber framing, and gravel under the deck. We treat those as the baseline for every Ward parcel regardless of which counter ends up reviewing it. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) meets the surface requirement, and at this altitude it also skips the refinishing clock that wood starts the first summer. Framing bays get closed or screened so embers cannot nest; the underside is not storage.

## Steep, rocky, and worth it

Self-built homes on rocky slopes rarely have a flat spot to put a deck, which is exactly why a deck is the best outdoor square footage on the lot. Stepped footings into rock, taller posts, and [railing](/services/railings) in slim aluminum or cable that keeps the forest and the canyon in view — the structure is the project, and we design from the actual grade.

## Old cabins, honest answers

Miners' cabins and decades of self-built additions mean plenty of existing decks here were framed to whatever lumber was on hand. We inspect first. A sound frame earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in ignition-resistant composite; an undersized one gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) on engineered, current-code structure. The answer comes in writing either way.

## Eighty-five minutes, honestly

From our Centennial shop, Ward is about eighty-five minutes — up past [Boulder](/locations/boulder) to the Peak to Peak, near the far edge of the range we serve and a bit beyond our Nederland work. Distance shapes scheduling, never the price or the [guarantee](/warranty). Most Ward builds get planned in fall and built once the ground opens in spring.
