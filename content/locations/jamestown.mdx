## A canyon town of a few dozen homes

Jamestown is about 250 people in James Creek canyon at 6,920 feet — a mining-era Main Street anchored by the Jamestown Mercantile, Elysian Park, and a few dozen cabins and cottages on narrow creekside lots with steep forested slopes rising on both sides. The September 2013 flood rebuilt much of the creek corridor, so the housing runs from century-old cottages to decade-old rebuilds, often on the same block. There is no flat ground to speak of, and a deck here is as much retaining structure and stair as it is floor.

## The county issues the permit

Jamestown is one of the few incorporated towns in Boulder County whose building permits come from the county rather than a town building department. The town's own permit page says so: new construction and remodels go through [Boulder County](/locations/boulder-county) Community Planning & Permitting, and county staff perform the plan reviews and inspections in town. Town Hall at 118 Main St. handles local permits other than building. We prepare the county's Residential Deck Requirements (B09) packet, submit it, and run inspections through final sign-off — one counter, and we know which one.

## Snow above the plains value

Boulder County's ground snow load map runs from 50 to 100 psf across the county, and the foothills around Jamestown sit above the 55 psf plains value, with the exact parcel figure confirmed with the county at plan review. That number sizes every beam, post, and footing, and it is the load a [covered section or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) has to carry as a roof. Footings go below the 30–36 inch frost line.

## Section R390 is the deck spec

County permits here fall under Boulder County's ignition-resistant construction requirements, Section R390 of the 2021 code amendments. For a deck that means noncombustible or approved composite decking, 2x or heavy-timber framing, and a weed barrier with 2 inches of 3/4-inch gravel beneath the deck extending 5 feet out from the house. Those are the details we would choose on a forested canyon lot regardless; in Jamestown they are the code. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) meets the surface requirement without the annual refinishing clock that wood starts at this elevation, and we detail the framing bays and underside so embers have nowhere to settle.

## Creekside lots and canyon walls

Narrow lots pinned between James Creek and the slope mean the deck often is the yard. Elevated platforms over the creek side, stepped footings into the hill, [railing](/services/railings) in slim aluminum or cable that keeps the canyon in view — the structure is the project, not the preamble, and we design from the actual grade rather than a plan-view sketch.

## Repair or replace, after an inspection

Post-flood rebuilds are often sound frames that simply need boards or railing, and those earn a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) for meaningfully less than starting over. Cabin-era decks built to lighter snow assumptions usually get a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation on current-code framing. We inspect first and put the answer in writing either way.

## Eighty minutes, honestly

From our Centennial shop, Jamestown is about eighty minutes — up through [Boulder](/locations/boulder) and into the foothills, at the far edge of the range we serve. Distance shapes how we schedule site visits and build weeks, never the price or the [guarantee](/warranty) behind the work.
