## The boomtown of the northern Front Range

Windsor went from about 9,000 people in 2000 to roughly 46,000 today, and the housing stock tells the story: more than a third of it was built between 2010 and 2019, nearly all of it detached single-family, most of it inside master-planned communities — Water Valley on its lakes and golf course, RainDance ranked among the nation's top-selling master plans, Greenspire, Highpointe Vista.

That's a town full of nearly new houses on open high-plains ground at about 4,800 feet, between Fort Collins and Greeley. It's also a town full of backyards the builder never finished.

## The builder-basic backyard problem

Production builders deliver a house; the outdoor space is usually a concrete stoop or the smallest deck that passes code. Five years in, that platform is undersized for the family, unshaded, and starting to show what Colorado does to bargain materials.

We design the replacement as one plan: a [composite deck](/services/composite-decks) sized for how the yard actually gets used, [railing](/services/railings) that doesn't fence off the space, and shade designed in rather than bolted on later. Starting from nearly nothing is genuinely the easier project — there's no bad structure to work around.

## Wind, taken seriously

Windsor knows wind better than most Colorado towns — the EF3 tornado of May 22, 2008 remains the costliest in state history, and the town still maintains an official page on it. We're not designing for tornadoes; nobody honestly can. But the everyday exposure here is real: open plains, no windbreaks, and gusts with a long run at your lot.

For a [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), that means the governing load is uplift — wind pulling the roof upward — not snow pressing down. Engineered post bases, hardware specified for the exposure, and footings sized accordingly are what keep the structure attached to the house.

## Hail country, and what to build with

Weld County sits squarely in hail alley — Colorado runs second in the nation for hail claims — and a mile-high sun bakes whatever the hail misses. That combination is the core of our composite-first argument: capped composite shrugs off UV and impact that leave softwood checked, splintered, and gray in a few seasons. If you're weighing the options, our [composite vs. hardwood comparison](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) lays out the honest trade-offs.

## What's under the lawn matters too

Much of Weld County sits on expansive bentonite clay — soil that swells when wet and shrinks when dry, heaving anything with shallow footings. Combined with Front Range freeze-thaw, it's why we drill proper caisson footings to full frost depth, 30 to 36 inches, on every build. On a new deck it's invisible. Ten years later it's the difference between level and not.

## Permits, districts, and paperwork

Deck and pergola permits inside town limits run through Town of Windsor Community Development, with SAFEbuilt performing inspections. Before that, most Windsor neighborhoods require metro-district or HOA architectural approval — and that review usually drives the schedule more than the permit does. We handle both packets and manage inspections through final; our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the general process.
