faqs:
  - q: "Who issues deck permits in Greeley?"
    a: "The City of Greeley handles permits inside city limits, and Weld County covers parcels outside them — two different counters with different submittals, which matters more here than in the metro because so many properties sit right at that boundary.   - q: "Do we need an engineered cover out here?"
    a: "If it has a roof, yes. There's a great deal of open farmland upwind of most Greeley neighborhoods, and an exposed roofed structure gets loaded upward — uplift — rather than simply pressed down by snow. That drives engineered post bases, hardware chosen for the exposure, and footings sized to match. Open pergolas are more forgiving than solid roofs, but both need designing for it."
  - q: "Our lot is big. What should we actually build?"
    a: "Something proportioned to the space, which usually means zones rather than one platform: a covered area for cooking and eating, an open area for sun, and often a lower level stepping toward the yard. The common mistake on a large lot is building a standard suburban deck that ends up looking undersized against everything around it."

## Weld County proportions

Greeley has never had to be stingy with space, and that shapes the work. Lots run generous, the horizon is wide open in most directions, and the newer neighborhoods on the edges of the city were laid out on farmland with the same relaxed relationship to acreage that the surrounding county has always had.

For deck design that's an opportunity and a trap in equal measure: you can build what the house actually wants rather than what fits between the setbacks, but a standard suburban platform dropped onto a Greeley lot reads as undersized, like furniture borrowed from a smaller house.

## Build zones, not a platform

The projects that work here are usually zoned — a covered section for cooking and eating, an open section for sun, and often a lower level stepping toward the yard.

[Built-in seating](/services/deck-replacement) earns its keep on lots like these, because people gather in numbers and nobody wants to carry chairs across a yard this size. Lighting matters just as much — the long evenings are the point of having the space. And for the surface itself, [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) is the straightforward answer: an unshaded deck out here takes full high-altitude UV all day, serious hail seasons, and sharp freeze-thaw swings.

## Wind is the design load

There's a lot of open farmland upwind of most Greeley neighborhoods, and anything with a roof over it gets loaded upward in a windstorm rather than pressed down by snow — so post bases, connections, and footings on a [pergola or solid patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) all get specified for uplift.

Solid roofs catch more wind than open pergolas and need it more. This is the same engineering we bring to [Brighton's](/locations/brighton) big lots and [Erie's](/locations/erie) prairie, and explains why a properly engineered cover costs more than the cheapest number you'll be quoted.

## Older neighborhoods, honest inspections

Greeley's established core has housing old enough that its decks are usually second-generation and overdue. Our approach doesn't change: inspect the structure before quoting the surface, starting with the ledger connection where the deck meets the house, because that's where the expensive failures live.

If the framing is genuinely sound, a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) puts a new composite surface on the structure you already own for meaningfully less. If it isn't, a full [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) on code-current framing is the right call and we'll explain exactly why. Written either way — including when the cheaper answer wins.

Where a lot opens west, [railing](/services/railings) choice is worth the spend: slim aluminum or cable keeps the Front Range on the horizon instead of behind a fence of pickets.

## City or county

Inside city limits the City of Greeley is your counter; outside them it's Weld County, and the two aren't interchangeable — worth confirming first, since so many Greeley properties sit close to that line. We do that, then produce drawings to the right standard, file the [permit](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro), and manage inspections through sign-off.

## The drive, plainly

Greeley is roughly an hour from our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial). So we take projects here that justify the trip — full builds and covered structures rather than small repairs we couldn't service well from that distance — and we plan the logistics before quoting.

Distance doesn't change the engineering, the [manufacturer certifications](/blog/trex-vs-timbertech-vs-fiberon) behind the materials, or the [written guarantee](/warranty) that covers the work. Try the [cost calculator](/deck-cost-calculator) or read the [cost guide](/blog/composite-deck-cost-denver) first if you'd like, then [tell us about your lot](/contact).
