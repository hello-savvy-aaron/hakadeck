## Ute Pass, at 7,400 feet

Cascade is where the Front Range stops being foothills. The community sits at about 7,398 feet along Fountain Creek, at the base of the Pikes Peak Highway, with Chipita Park next door and US-24 threading the canyon. The housing is cabins and 1960s–2000s mountain homes on steep, forested lots, and a good share of it is seasonal — places people open in May and close after the aspens turn. Almost every deck here is doing structural work: hanging off a slope, reaching for the one patch of sun, or carrying a roof that has to hold real snow.

That is the design brief we build to here.

## Permits: county planning first, then PPRBD

Cascade and Chipita Park are unincorporated [El Paso County](/locations/el-paso-county), so there is no town hall. The building permit comes from the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department, the same regional counter that serves [Colorado Springs](/locations/colorado-springs) and Monument, while setback and floodplain questions along Fountain Creek sometimes need separate approval from County Planning and Community Development. We confirm the parcel, sort the county step from the regional step, and manage inspections through final.

## Snow that stays

Cascade is above the 7,000-foot line where PPRBD's design criteria step up: 57 psf ground snow and 40 psf flat-roof snow, versus 43/30 down in the Springs. The number matters most on a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), where the roof, beams, posts, and footings all get sized to it. The canyon adds its own weight: heavy shade, north-facing slopes, and long freeze periods mean snow that lands on a deck in December can still be there in March. Older framing built to a lighter assumption is worth a look before the next big winter. Footings go below the 30–36 inch frost line.

## Composite, for a shaded canyon

A shaded, damp, freeze-thaw canyon is hard on wood. Finishes fail early, boards check and cup, and the annual staining schedule becomes the deck's real cost of ownership. That is why we lead with [capped composite](/services/composite-decks): the polymer cap handles moisture, ice, and the altitude UV that reaches the open spots, and maintenance drops to soap and water. On lots this steep, a slim aluminum or cable railing keeps the creek and the peak in view instead of behind pickets.

## Cabin decks: repair or replace

Plenty of Cascade decks date to the cabin era, framed before the current snow tier and hardware standards. We inspect the structure before quoting and answer plainly. A sound frame earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite at $20–$35 per square foot, well under a teardown. An undersized frame, a rotted ledger, or footings that never reached frost depth get a straight [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation, in writing, because rebuilding once beats repairing twice at this elevation. We photograph what we find so you can see the reasoning.

## Eighty minutes, in blocks

From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), Cascade is about eighty minutes — down I-25, then up US-24 through Ute Pass toward [Green Mountain Falls](/locations/green-mountain-falls) and Woodland Park. We schedule mountain work in blocks, batching site visits and running build days back to back. Price and the warranty don't change with the mile marker — lead time does.
