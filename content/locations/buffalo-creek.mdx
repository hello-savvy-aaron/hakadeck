## A hamlet inside the burn scar

Buffalo Creek is about a hundred people on County Road 126 south of Pine Grove, at roughly 6,700 feet on the North Fork of the South Platte — historic cabins, a few newer homes, and the Buffalo Creek Recreation Area, all surrounded by Pike National Forest and the scar of the 1996 Buffalo Creek Fire. That history is visible from most porches. It is also the clearest argument anywhere in Jefferson County for building a deck that does not feed a fire, and since mid-2026 the county code says the same thing.

## Permits: Jefferson County Building Safety

Buffalo Creek is unincorporated Jefferson County at the southern edge of the county, so Jeffco Building Safety at 100 Jefferson County Parkway in Golden issues the deck permit, with applications and inspection requests through the county's CitizenPortal. Since July 1, 2026, the county's Wildfire Resiliency Code applies to permits in unincorporated Jeffco: on parcels inside the mapped WUI overlay, deck surfaces must be noncombustible, framing must limit ignition, fencing within eight feet of a structure must be nonflammable, and nothing flammable may sit within five feet. The rule covers exterior alterations such as deck replacements and additions, not only new homes. Map status is parcel-specific, and we confirm it before designing. Our permit guide covers the process, and the [Jefferson County](/locations/jefferson-county) page covers the county.

## Snow at 6,700 feet, footings at 36 inches

Jefferson County tiers ground snow load by elevation — 43 psf below 6,500 feet, 54 psf at 7,000 — so Buffalo Creek parcels land around 46–49 psf. That number sizes every beam and post under a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers). Frost depth is 36 inches, wind is designed at 129 mph in Exposure B, and the county classes weathering as severe. Riverside lots add their own detail: uneven ground and a damp microclimate along the North Fork, which is hard on softwood and on unrated hardware. Footings go to frost, connectors are rated for freeze-thaw, and our post on how much snow a deck can hold explains the load arithmetic.

## Fire-aware first

On a WUI-mapped parcel, [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) or mineral-based decking is the code-aligned surface, and in Buffalo Creek we would recommend it even where the map did not require it. A deck is one of the most ember-exposed assemblies on a house. We frame bays screened or closed so embers cannot nest, keep the underside clear of stored fuel, and choose railing that keeps the river and the forest in view without adding flammable mass to the edge of the house. None of it makes a deck fireproof; it makes the deck meaningfully less of a liability. Our composite vs. hardwood comparison is honest about what real wood gives up here.

## Cabin decks, honestly assessed

Most existing decks in Buffalo Creek went on historic cabins, to snow loads and hardware standards the county no longer accepts. We inspect the frame before quoting. Sound framing earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) at $20–$35 per square foot over the existing structure; framing that has aged out gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) on current-code structure, which on mapped parcels must carry a noncombustible surface anyway. The recommendation comes in writing, and the deck safety inspection guide shows what we check.

Buffalo Creek is the far end of our Jefferson County range — about seventy minutes from our [Centennial](/locations/centennial) shop, up US-285 past [Conifer](/locations/conifer) and down through [Pine](/locations/pine) Grove on County Road 126.
