## A small town growing fast

Mead sits on open high plains at the crossing of I-25 and Highway 66 — a Weld County town of about 3,400 people in 2010 that has since nearly doubled to roughly 6,450. Almost all of that growth is new construction: Highlands and Highlands Preserve, Liberty Ranch, Coyote Run, Sorrento, and more building out around them. The dominant housing stock here is post-2015, and it shares one trait almost universally — the backyard came builder-basic. A back door, a step to grade, a fence, and dirt.

That blank slate is most of our work in Mead.

## The clean-sheet deck

Building on a new home is the easy case, and we prefer it to inheriting someone's 1990s compromises. We start from proper footings below Colorado frost depth, size the layout to the household instead of the brochure, and make the materials decision once. Where a subdivision HOA runs design review, we prepare that submittal alongside the town permit so the two move together instead of in sequence.

## Shade is a design problem here

There is no mature canopy on the plains — every one of these neighborhoods is a decade or less into growing its trees — so shade on a Mead deck has to be built. A [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) turns a west-facing deck from a place you abandon at 4 p.m. into a room you use all evening, and on open ground it has to be engineered for wind uplift rather than borrowed from a catalog. Done while the deck footings are already going in, it is dramatically cheaper than retrofitting later; the same logic applies to running gas for a future [outdoor kitchen](/services/outdoor-kitchens).

## Why composite on the high plains

Mead's exposure list is the standard Front Range set, undiluted by tree cover: full-strength UV at altitude, hail, wind-driven grit, and hard freeze-thaw swings. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) handles all of it with soap and water, where wood signs you up for a refinishing cycle that starts the year the deck is built. On a low-maintenance new house, that is the consistent choice — and our [materials comparison](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) is honest about the cases where wood still wins. Slim aluminum [railings](/services/railings) round it out with the same zero-upkeep logic.

## Repair and replacement, when it applies

Most of Mead is too new for failing decks, but the older streets near the town core carry wood structures decades into their run. Those get inspected before they get quoted: sound framing earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair), failing framing gets a current-code [replacement](/services/deck-replacement), and either answer comes in writing — including when the cheaper one is right.

## Permits, done online

The Town of Mead Building Division is staffed through SAFEbuilt and runs permitting online-only through its CommunityConnect portal. That is a stumbling block for builders who have never worked here and a non-event for us: drawings, portal submittal, and inspections are part of every build. Our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the general process if you want the background.
