## The top of our service area, literally

Breckenridge sits at 9,600 feet, and many of its homes sit above 10,000. The town is a Victorian mining camp turned resort: 1880s cottages in the National Historic District along Main Street, condo complexes at the Peak 7 and Peak 8 base areas, the Wellington Neighborhood, and large custom log-and-timber homes on forested lots in Warriors Mark, the Highlands at Breckenridge, and along Boreas Pass Road. It is also the heaviest snow load we design for anywhere, written into town code.

## Ninety to one hundred pounds per square foot

Breckenridge Town Code 8-1-4 amends the 2024 IBC with a design roof snow load of 90 psf below 10,000 feet and 100 psf at or above it, with no reduction allowed. Frost protection goes 40 inches below finish grade. And roofs must be designed or fitted with snow-shed barriers so snow does not shed onto decks, balconies, or exits — a rule that exists because a roof avalanche onto a deck is a structural event.

For a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), that means beams, posts, and footings roughly double what a Denver-metro cover carries. For an uncovered deck under an eave, we look at what the roof above can drop and frame for it. Our guide to [how much snow a deck can hold](/blog/how-much-snow-can-my-deck-hold) explains why 100 psf is not a number to round.

## Permits through the Building Division

The Town of Breckenridge Building Division issues deck permits, with applications, inspection scheduling, and payments running through the Building Access Portal on Tyler EnerGov. Historic District properties are subject to town design standards, and the Highlands and Warriors Mark add HOA architectural review, which we complete before filing. Parcels outside town limits, including parts of the Blue River valley, permit through the Summit County Building Inspection Department in Frisco instead.

Breckenridge is Red, White & Blue Fire territory in Summit County's moderate-to-high fire-intensity area. The Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code — Class 2 hardening, ignition-resistant deck surfaces — applies to county permits after May 4, 2026, and we confirm the town's adoption status on every job. The [Summit County page](/locations/summit-county) has the county-wide rules.

## Composite, timber-house scale

On Warriors Mark and Boreas Pass lots, the deck is often the biggest outdoor room on a big house, and the surface has to survive altitude UV, months of snowpack, and freeze-thaw that would shred wood in two seasons. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) does that, and where the ignition-resistant surface rule applies it is the code-aligned choice. Slim aluminum or cable railing keeps the Tenmile Range in view, and Breckenridge is a certified Dark Sky Community, keeping deck lighting low and shielded.

## Repair or replace

Decks built before the 90/100 psf amendment were often framed to assumptions the town no longer accepts. We inspect the structure and answer in writing: sound framing earns a [repair or re-deck](/services/deck-repair); an undersized frame gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) built to current code. We photograph what a Breckenridge winter has done to the ledger and show you.

## Close to two hours, scheduled in blocks

From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), Breckenridge is close to two hours via I-70 and the Eisenhower Tunnel, the far end of our Summit County work past [Frisco](/locations/frisco) and Dillon. We schedule mountain builds in blocks so the crew stays on site, coordinating with property managers and HOA contacts along the way. The drive changes nothing about the guarantee.
