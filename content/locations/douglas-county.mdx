## The county between the cities

Douglas County fills the ground between Denver and Colorado Springs — Highlands Ranch and Lone Tree pressed against the metro at the north end, Castle Rock in the middle, and the Palmer Divide rising toward Larkspur at the south. The housing splits the same way: some of the largest master-planned communities in Colorado, built mostly in the last thirty years, and ponderosa-covered acreage in the unincorporated country between and beyond them. Both ends keep us busy, for different reasons.

## Where we build in Douglas County

[Castle Rock](/locations/castle-rock) is the county seat and our busiest Douglas County town, full of builder-grade decks hitting replacement age; [Parker](/locations/parker) is much the same story a few miles east. [Highlands Ranch](/locations/highlands-ranch) is the county's biggest community and runs everything through HRCA architectural review before the permit. [Lone Tree](/locations/lone-tree) brings smaller, higher-end lots where the railing choice carries the design, and [Castle Pines](/locations/castle-pines) sits in the pines with HOA review on nearly every street. Further out, [Roxborough Park](/locations/roxborough-park) backs up to red rock and real wildfire exposure, [Larkspur](/locations/larkspur) puts us on the Divide itself — Perry Park and Sage Port included — and [Franktown](/locations/franktown) is acreage country where the deck is often the best room on the property.

## Permits: one county, five town counters

The Douglas County Building Division issues permits for unincorporated addresses — Roxborough Park, Franktown, Sedalia, most Larkspur addresses including Perry Park and Sage Port, and Highlands Ranch, where the HRCA wants architectural review completed before you submit. Castle Rock, Parker, Lone Tree, Castle Pines, and the town core of Larkspur each run their own building departments with their own submittal habits. The county recommends HOA or architectural committee approval before applying, and we treat that as the schedule-setter it usually is. We confirm the right counter for your address, prepare one clean packet, and manage inspections through final — our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the background.

## Snow by elevation, footings by frost

Douglas County sets design snow load by elevation: a 30 psf floor that rises as the ground climbs toward the Palmer Divide. That is not paperwork trivia — it sizes every beam and post under a [covered deck or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), and it is why a Sage Port structure carries more steel and lumber than the same footprint in Lone Tree. Frost depth runs 30 to 36 inches countywide, which sets footing depth on every project. We engineer to the numbers for the parcel.

## Master plans and ponderosa acreage

At the metro end, the work is mostly replacement: thousands of production homes shipped with minimum-spec wood decks that are now sagging on schedule, and swapping one for an engineered [composite deck](/services/composite-decks) is the most common quote we write here. On the acreage side, projects get bigger and more structural — walkouts, covered sections, [railing](/services/railings) chosen to keep the view. Where an existing frame is sound we say so and price a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair); where it is not, a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) built once beats repairing twice.
