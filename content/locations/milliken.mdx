## A farm town with a subdivision ring

Milliken sits on CO 60 between Johnstown and Greeley, about 4,760 feet up on flat farm ground near the Big Thompson River. The old town is the early-1900s grid along Broad Street; around it, the 2000s and 2010s added Settlers Village, Colony Pointe, and Centennial Farms — production two-stories and ranches on level lots with not much between them and the horizon. Roughly 9,000 people live here now, and most of the housing is under twenty-five years old.

That split shapes the work. Downtown, we are usually replacing an older wood deck or a small porch on a tight lot. In the subdivisions, we are replacing the builder-grade deck that came with the house, or building the first real one where the builder left a step to grade.

## Permits go through Community Development

The Town of Milliken's Community Development department issues building permits, and the process is online-only through CommunityCore. A deck is not a quick-counter permit here: the town requires a full residential submittal with scaled plans, and it quotes plan review at 10 to 14 business days. That is longer than some neighboring towns, and it is the number to plan around. We draw the plans, file through the portal, and run inspections through final as part of every build. Addresses outside town limits file with [Weld County](/locations/weld-county) instead, and we confirm which applies to your parcel before anything is drawn.

## Wind, hail, frost, sun

Milliken does not publish its own design criteria, so the regional Weld County values govern: 30 psf ground snow, 115 mph three-second-gust wind, and a 30-inch frost depth. Snow is the easy number on the plains. Wind is the one that matters — a [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) here is engineered for uplift, with post bases and connectors sized so the roof stays attached in a spring gale. Frost depth sets every footing, and the clay-heavy farm soils along the Big Thompson are one more reason we drill to full depth rather than trusting a shallow pad.

Then there is the sun and the hail. A south-facing deck at this elevation takes UV that strips a wood finish in a couple of seasons, and Weld County is hail country. Both are the argument for [capped composite](/services/composite-decks), which takes the impact and the sun without a refinishing schedule. If you are still weighing wood, we will lay out both sides honestly at the site visit.

## What we build here

The typical Milliken project is a 300–400 square foot composite deck on a subdivision lot, often with a covered section so it is usable through the shoulder seasons and shaded in July. Where the lot is flat and the door sits near grade, the structure is straightforward and the budget goes into the surface, the [railing](/services/railings), and the shade. Where a home has a walkout, the deck goes up a story and the engineering follows. We are a Trex, TimberTech, and Deckorators dealer, and every build carries our two-year workmanship warranty.

## Repair or replace

Settlers Village and Colony Pointe decks are hitting the age where surface boards cup and railings loosen while the frame underneath is often still fine. We inspect first — ledger, posts, footings, hardware — and give you the honest answer. Sound framing gets a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) at $20–$35 per square foot; a failed ledger or shallow footings get a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) plan. The findings go to you in writing, with photos.
