## Nine thousand feet, and it shows

Winter Park sits at 9,052 feet at the head of the Fraser Valley — a ski-resort town of roughly a thousand year-round residents and a great many more second homes. The housing runs three ways: condo and townhome clusters near the base, mountain-modern single-family homes on forested lots in Rendezvous and Winter Park Highlands, and older A-frames and cabins in Old Town and along Vasquez Road. Heavy snow and short summers decide the design up here. A deck that works in Winter Park is a structure first and an outdoor room second.

## Permits: the Winter Park–Fraser Building Division

The Town of Winter Park runs its own building division, and it also administers permits, plan review, and inspections for neighboring [Fraser](/locations/fraser). The office is at 50 Vasquez Road, Suite B, reached at 970-726-8081, and applications go through the town's Accela portal. The town is on the 2021 I-Codes with local amendments, and in June 2026 it adopted the 2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code, so deck surfaces and exterior materials have to meet its ignition-resistance provisions. In Rendezvous and the Highlands, an architectural review committee sees the design before the town does. We handle the review packet, the permit, and the inspections through final; our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) explains how we run the process.

## The town writes the snow number into code

Winter Park's amendments are specific in ways most Front Range towns are not. Uncovered decks and balconies must be designed for a 60 psf live load or the design snow load for the site, whichever is greater, and no snow-load duration factors are allowed. Ground snow loads come from the SEAC Colorado Design Snow Loads report. The frost line is 30 inches below finished grade, and the minimum design wind speed is 105 mph. Roofs must not shed snow onto decks — metal roofs need snow-shed barriers protecting decks and exits — which governs where a [covered section](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) can go and how the roof above it is detailed. None of that is paperwork trivia. It sizes every beam, post, and footing we set.

## What we build here

[Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) is our default in Winter Park for the same reasons the wildfire code favors it: it does not feed embers the way untreated softwood does, and it stands up to altitude UV and months under snowpack without starting an annual refinishing clock. Framing gets sized to the design load for the parcel, not to a metro detail. Railing is chosen to keep the view — slim aluminum or cable on the forested view lots in the Highlands, sturdier composite where a family wants something to lean on. Where a homeowner wants a hardwood surface, we say honestly what the maintenance looks like at 9,000 feet before we quote it.

## Repair or replace

Old Town cabins and older balconies often carry decks built to lighter assumptions than the current 60 psf minimum. We inspect the frame first and photograph what we find. Sound structure gets a [repair or re-deck](/services/deck-repair); undersized posts, beams, or footings get a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) built to the town's numbers. Either answer comes in writing, with the reasoning.
