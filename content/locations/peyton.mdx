## Big sky, no shade

Peyton itself is a crossroads of a few hundred people on the plains east of Colorado Springs, but the Peyton ZIP covers a lot more than that: the fast-growing Falcon area, with Meridian Ranch, Woodmen Hills, and Paint Brush Hills full of 2000s–2020s production homes, plus 5-acre ranchettes out toward the Black Forest edge. The lots are flat, windy, and treeless, with expansive soils underneath and a horizon in every direction. It is the opposite of our Ute Pass work a half hour west — here the deck is not fighting a slope or a snowbank. It is fighting the wind and the sun.

## Permits: one regional counter, plus the HOA

Peyton and Falcon are unincorporated [El Paso County](/locations/el-paso-county), so there is no town hall. The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department issues the building permit, the same counter that serves [Colorado Springs](/locations/colorado-springs) and Monument, and El Paso County Planning and Community Development handles setback and site questions. Decks are permit-required, and PPRBD's homeowner permits are limited to owner-occupied primary residences, so in practice the licensed contractor pulls it. Many Falcon subdivisions — Meridian Ranch and Woodmen Hills among them — layer HOA design review on top, and we complete that before the permit goes in because it is usually what sets the schedule.

## Wind first, then snow

At about 6,801 feet Peyton is below the 7,000-foot line, so PPRBD's design criteria are 43 psf ground snow and 30 psf flat-roof snow — real, but not the mountain tier. The number that actually drives the engineering here is wind. With no tree cover, a [patio cover or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) takes the full force of it, and uplift will pull an under-anchored roof apart before snow ever gets a chance. We engineer covered structures for uplift as well as gravity, tie them into properly sized footings, and set those footings below the 30–36 inch frost line in soils that heave. A roof built that way costs more than a catalog kit, and it is still standing after the next spring windstorm.

## Composite, for a lot with no shade

Full-sun exposure at 6,800 feet, Front Range hail, and daily freeze-thaw through the shoulder seasons: wood finishes fail fast out here, and the staining schedule becomes the deck's real cost. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) handles UV and hail with soap and water, which is why we lead with it. Wood still has a place where you want the grain and accept the upkeep; out here, most owners do not. And with the view running to the horizon, a slim aluminum or cable railing keeps it that way.

## Builder decks: repair or replace

Most decks around Falcon are builder-grade wood on production homes, and the pattern is consistent: the frame is often fine and the surface is not. We inspect first. A sound ledger, posts, and footings earn a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite at $20–$35 per square foot; heaved footings or a minimum-spec frame that never suited this wind get a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation in writing. Either way you get both numbers side by side, with photos of what we found under the boards.

From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), Peyton is about seventy-five minutes — I-25 south, then east across the north side of the Springs. We say that plainly, and we schedule the Pikes Peak region in blocks, batching site visits and build days rather than making one-off trips.
