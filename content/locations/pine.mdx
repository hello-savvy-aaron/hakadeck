## Two elevations, one name

Pine is really two places. Pine Grove sits at about 6,800 feet on the North Fork of the South Platte, a historic railroad town of 1880s cottages beside Pine Valley Ranch Park, the Jeffco Open Space park at the end of the road. Pine Junction is 1,700 feet higher on Highway 285, at roughly 8,500 feet, where 1970s-to-2000s mountain homes sit on large wooded lots through Woodside and out toward Crystal Lake and Elk Falls. Around 2,000 people live across the whole area, and the deck that suits a Pine Grove cottage is not the deck that suits a Pine Junction lot — the snow load nearly doubles on the climb.

## Permits: Jefferson County Building Safety

All of Pine is unincorporated Jefferson County, so Jeffco Building Safety at 100 Jefferson County Parkway in Golden issues the deck permit, with applications and inspection requests through the county's CitizenPortal. Since July 1, 2026, the county's Wildfire Resiliency Code applies to permits in unincorporated Jeffco: on parcels inside the mapped WUI overlay, deck surfaces must be noncombustible and framing must limit ignition, and that reaches exterior alterations such as deck replacements and additions. Map status is parcel-specific, and we check your address before we draw. Our permit guide covers the process, and the [Jefferson County](/locations/jefferson-county) page covers the county.

## Forty-nine psf at the bottom, ninety-seven at the top

Jefferson County sets ground snow load by elevation, and Pine spans most of the table: about 49 psf near Pine Grove, 67 psf at 7,500 feet, 82 psf at 8,000, and 97 psf near Pine Junction. The county's rule that any ground snow load above 70 psf must be designed by a registered design professional lands squarely on the upper half of Pine, which means most Pine Junction [covered decks and patio covers](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) are engineer-stamped by requirement, not preference. Frost depth is 36 inches, wind is designed at 129 mph in Exposure B, and weathering is classed severe. We pull the figure for your parcel and size every beam, post, and footing to it; our post on how much snow a deck can hold shows the arithmetic.

## What we build along the North Fork and up 285

In Pine Grove, the work is usually a modest deck on a historic cottage, sized to a small lot and detailed to look right beside 1880s siding. Up toward Pine Junction, it is larger — walkouts on steep lots, covered sections that make the deck usable in winter, railing that keeps the ridgelines in view. Either way, [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) is the code-aligned surface on WUI-mapped parcels and the practical one everywhere here, since altitude UV and freeze-thaw start an annual refinishing clock on softwood. We frame fire-aware regardless of map status: bays screened against embers, undersides kept clear of stored fuel. The covered deck cost guide has honest numbers for the roofed version.

## Repair or replace, by elevation

Older Pine decks were built to lighter snow assumptions than the current county table, and the higher the parcel, the wider the gap. We inspect before quoting. Sound framing earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) at $20–$35 per square foot; framing that cannot carry the parcel's load gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) on current-code structure, which on mapped parcels must carry a noncombustible surface anyway. The recommendation comes in writing, and the inspection guide shows what we look at.
