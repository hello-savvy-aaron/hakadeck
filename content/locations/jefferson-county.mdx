## Two counties in one

Jefferson County is really two places wearing one name. On the flats, it is Denver's western suburbs — Lakewood, Arvada, Wheat Ridge — built out largely between the 1960s and the 1980s, with mature trees and a huge stock of original wood decks. In the foothills, it is forested, steep, and fire-mapped: Evergreen, Conifer, Morrison's canyon neighborhoods, climbing to 8,000 feet and beyond. The two halves need different decks, and since mid-2026 they answer to meaningfully different rules.

## Where we build in Jefferson County

[Lakewood](/locations/lakewood) is the county's biggest city and steady replacement work; [Golden](/locations/golden) sits against the mesas where wind starts mattering. [Arvada](/locations/arvada) and [Wheat Ridge](/locations/wheat-ridge) are classic 1960s-to-80s suburbs full of decks aging out together. Up the hill, [Evergreen](/locations/evergreen) and [Conifer](/locations/conifer) are true mountain building — snow loads, steep lots, and WUI-mapped parcels — while [Morrison](/locations/morrison) straddles the transition and [Ken Caryl](/locations/ken-caryl) tucks its valley neighborhoods right against the hogback.

## Permits: Jeffco and five city counters

Jeffco Building Safety issues permits for unincorporated addresses — Ken Caryl, Conifer, most Morrison-area addresses, and Evergreen — while Lakewood, Golden, Arvada, Wheat Ridge, and Westminster run their own counters with their own habits. We confirm the jurisdiction for your parcel, prepare one clean packet, and carry it through inspections. The background is in our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro).

## The wildfire code changed the default

The biggest recent change in county deck building: Jefferson County's Wildfire Resiliency Code took effect July 1, 2026. In mapped WUI zones, new and replacement deck surfaces must be noncombustible, with ignition-limiting framing underneath. It is parcel-specific — we check the map for every foothills address before drawing — but the practical upshot is that on mapped parcels, untreated softwood is off the table and [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) is the code-aligned choice. We were building fire-aware in these hills before the code required it — framing bays screened against embers, undersides kept clear of stored fuel — and we think the county got this one right.

## Foothills structure, flatland replacement

Mountain parcels here ask for real engineering: snow loads that size every beam under a [covered deck](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), frost-depth footings, and walkout structures that make steep lots livable. Down on the flats, the work is mostly the replacement cycle — 1960s-to-80s wood decks whose frames we inspect before quoting anything. Sound framing gets a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair); finished framing gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) on current-code structure, and we put that answer in writing either way. If you want to gauge your own deck first, start with the [deck safety inspection guide](/deck-safety-inspection-guide).

## The drive, honestly

From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), the county's near edge is about half an hour and the high foothills closer to an hour. That shapes scheduling — we batch mountain site visits, and fall contracts win the spring calendar, as our guide to the [best time to build in Colorado](/blog/best-time-to-build-deck-colorado) explains. It does not shape the price or the [guarantee](/warranty).
