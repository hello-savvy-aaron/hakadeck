## A town that is almost all new

Severance sits just north of Windsor at about 4,920 feet, on flat ground that was farmland twenty years ago. It is one of the fastest-growing statutory towns in Colorado, and the housing shows it: outside a small pre-war core along 1st Street, nearly everything is a 2005–2025 production two-story or ranch in Hidden Valley Farm, Tailholt, Belmont Ridge, The Overlook, or Summit View. Roughly 9,000 people live here now.

The backyards came the way production backyards do — a back door, a step or a small code-minimum wood deck, a fence, and open sky. That blank slate is most of our work in Severance.

## Permits, SAFEbuilt, and the HOA

The Town of Severance Building Department issues building permits, with inspections contracted to SAFEbuilt Colorado. Two things make the town different from its neighbors. Applications are accepted online only, through Community Core, and the town requires a contractor license for any work inside town limits, which we hold.

The HOA is the other gate. Nearly every subdivision here has architectural review for decks and fences, and the committee's meeting schedule often sets the timeline more than the town does. We prepare the HOA package from the same drawings we file for the permit and run both in parallel. Addresses outside town limits file with [Weld County](/locations/weld-county) instead.

## Open-plains engineering

Severance does not publish its own design criteria, so the regional Weld County values apply: 30 psf ground snow, 115 mph three-second-gust wind, and a 30-inch frost depth. On flat, treeless ground the wind gets a long run at every lot, so a [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) is engineered for uplift, with post bases and connectors chosen so the roof stays on. Footings go below frost, drilled rather than padded, because the clay under these former fields heaves anything shallow.

Sun and hail finish the case for [capped composite](/services/composite-decks). A south-facing deck at this elevation takes UV that strips a wood finish in two seasons, and Weld County is hail country. Composite takes both without a refinishing schedule, which is the right match for a house where everything else is already low-maintenance. If you are still weighing wood, we will lay out both sides honestly at the site visit.

## Shade is the design problem

There is not a mature tree for half a mile in most of these subdivisions, so a bare composite deck is a hot deck in July. The typical Severance project pairs a 300–400 square foot deck with a pergola or a solid cover, designed as one structure rather than bolted on later. [Railing](/services/railings) choice matters more than people expect on these lots — slim aluminum keeps the open view, and it matches the HOA palettes we see most often. We price the cover as its own line so you can see exactly what the shade costs.

## The first builder decks are aging

The earliest Severance homes are now approaching twenty years old, and the minimum-spec wood decks that shipped with them are cupping and loosening on schedule. We inspect the frame before quoting. Sound structure gets a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite at $20–$35 per square foot; shallow footings or a failed ledger get a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) built to current code.
