    a: "The City of Louisville Building Safety Division — and the notable local wrinkle is that decks structurally attached to an existing home must be engineered by a Colorado-licensed structural engineer. That is a real requirement, not a formality, and builders who treat it as a surprise lose weeks to it.   - q: "What does a deck cost in Louisville?"
    a: "Capped composite runs about $40–$70 per square foot installed, which puts a typical 300–400 square foot Louisville deck around $15,000–$30,000 with railing and stairs. The engineering requirement for attached decks adds a modest, predictable line item rather than a surprise. Our online calculator gives you a planning range in about thirty seconds."
  - q: "We rebuilt after the Marshall Fire — how does that change a deck project?"
    a: "Mostly in ways that work in your favor: the house is new, the framing and finishes are known quantities, and the deck can be designed as part of the home rather than bolted on decades later. What we add is fire-aware detailing as a default — composite or mineral-based surfaces instead of untreated softwood, framing bays that embers cannot nest in, and an underside that is not designed as storage. On this ground, we consider that baseline practice, not an upgrade."
  - q: "Do you repair older decks, or only build new ones?"
    a: "Both. Old Town cottages and the 1990s homes around Coal Creek Ranch carry plenty of original wood decks, and we inspect the structure before quoting anything. Sound framing earns a re-deck in new composite for meaningfully less than starting over; a failing ledger or heaved footings make replacement the honest call, and we put that answer in writing either way."

## A town with two housing stocks

Louisville's housing tells its history in layers: miners' cottages and bungalows in Old Town, 1990s golf-course homes around Coal Creek Ranch, and newer construction in North End, Steel Ranch, and DELO. Since 2022 there is a fourth layer, and it is a large one.

The Marshall Fire of December 30, 2021 destroyed roughly 550 homes in Louisville — about 1,084 across the fire area — and the town's response was remarkable: through an expedited rebuild permitting program, roughly two-thirds of those homes were rebuilt within three years, far above the national average for disaster recovery. The result is a big cohort of brand-new houses, many of which are still waiting on the outdoor living that did not make the rebuild budget.

## Finishing the rebuild

For those households, a deck project is the last unfinished room of the house. The good news is that building on a 2022-or-newer home is the easy case — known framing, known finishes, no archaeology. What we bring is the discipline this ground has earned: [capped composite or mineral-based surfaces](/services/composite-decks) instead of untreated softwood, framing bays closed or screened so embers cannot nest, and an underside that is not a storage bay. The same logic applies one town over in [Superior](/locations/superior), and we treat it as the default, not a premium.

## The engineer requirement

Louisville has a local rule worth knowing before you collect bids: a deck structurally attached to an existing home must be engineered by a Colorado-licensed structural engineer. Builders who discover this at plan review lose weeks. We design to it from the first drawing, coordinate the stamp, and price it as the ordinary line item it is. Add it to the list of [questions to ask your deck builder](/questions-to-ask-your-deck-builder) — the answer tells you who has actually worked here.

## Wind country, still

Southeast Boulder County catches genuine Chinook windstorms coming off the foothills, which matters most for anything with a roof. A [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) here is loaded upward in a wind event, so engineered post bases, uplift-rated hardware, and properly sized footings are what keep it attached to the house. We build to the exposure, not to a catalog detail.

## Older decks, honest answers

Old Town and the 1990s neighborhoods carry plenty of original wood decks reaching the end of their run. We inspect the structure before quoting: sound framing earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in new composite, failing framing gets a current-code [replacement](/services/deck-replacement), and the recommendation comes in writing either way. Our [materials comparison](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) is honest about the trade-offs if you are still weighing surfaces.

## Permits and distance

Permits run through the City of Louisville Building Safety Division, and we handle drawings, filing, and inspections as part of every build — the general process is covered in our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro). From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial) Louisville is about forty minutes, which shapes scheduling, not price or the [guarantee](/warranty) behind the work.
