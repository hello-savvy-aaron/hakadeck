  - q: "Who issues deck permits in Roxborough Park?"

  - q: "What does a deck cost in Roxborough Park?"
    a: "Capped composite runs about $40–$70 per square foot installed, putting a typical 300–400 square foot deck around $15,000–$30,000 with railing and stairs. Steep, rocky Roxborough lots can push the structural side of that range — taller posts, engineered footings in rock, more stairs — and we price that honestly from the site rather than discovering it mid-build."
  - q: "Does wildfire risk change how you build here?"
    a: "Yes. Roxborough Park is a wildfire-interface community bordering open state park land, with one guarded road in and out — the exposure is not theoretical. We treat fire-aware detailing as the default: capped composite or mineral-based surfaces rather than untreated softwood, framing bays closed or screened so embers cannot nest, and deck undersides designed not to become storage for fuel. It does not make a deck fireproof, and we will not claim it does, but it meaningfully reduces what a deck contributes to the risk."
  - q: "Can you build on a steep, rocky lot?"
    a: "That is most of Roxborough Park, so yes. Steep lots change the structure more than the surface: taller posts with engineered bracing, footings designed for rock rather than soil, and stair runs that work with the grade instead of fighting it. Walkout and elevated decks are often the best square footage on these lots — the structure below is where the engineering lives, and it is not the place to economize."

## Building at the edge of the red rocks

Roxborough Park is a gated custom-home community set directly into the red-rock formations bordering Roxborough State Park, at about 5,900 feet. The lots are steep, rocky, and dramatic; the homes are custom, mostly in the $650K-to-$1.3M-plus range; and the whole community sits behind a single guarded access road. Around it, Roxborough Village, Ravenna, and Sterling Ranch fill in the valley — but inside the gate, this is one of the most distinctive places to build a deck in the south metro.

Distinctive terrain means the deck earns its engineering. Here is how we approach it.

## Steep lots put the money underneath

On a flat suburban lot, most of a deck budget is visible. On a Roxborough lot, a real share of it is structure: taller posts with engineered bracing, footings designed for rock rather than soil, and stair runs that negotiate the grade. Elevated and walkout decks are often the best usable square footage on these sites — our [second-story deck cost guide](/blog/second-story-deck-cost-colorado) explains why the structure below drives the number. We design from the actual site, not a catalog span table.

## Fire-aware as the default

This is a wildfire-interface community with open parkland on its border and one road out. We build accordingly, without being asked: [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) or mineral-based surfaces instead of untreated softwood, framing bays closed or screened so embers cannot nest beneath the deck, and undersides designed so they do not accumulate stored fuel. None of that makes a deck fireproof. It makes it a smaller liability, which on this ground is worth doing deliberately.

## Composite for the exposure, railing for the view

The same lots that carry fire exposure carry the rest of Colorado's abuse: intense UV at altitude, freeze-thaw through every winter, hail most summers. Composite handles all of it with near-zero maintenance, and the [railing](/services/railings) choice matters more here than most places — on a red-rock view lot, a slim or cable-style rail is the difference between framing the formations and fencing them off. A [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) engineered for wind and snow extends the season without blocking the sightlines.

## Permits, design review, and the gate

Roxborough Park is unincorporated Douglas County, and the county Building Division issues the permit — it maintains a dedicated deck-permit page and recommends HOA approval come first. The Roxborough Park Foundation runs that design review under its own Design Review Document, and contractor access through the guarded gate follows the Foundation's protocols. We sequence all three so the build never idles at the gatehouse. Our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the county process in detail.

## Repair or replace, answered honestly

If you have an existing deck, we inspect the frame before recommending anything. Sound structure gets a [repair](/services/deck-repair); aged-out structure gets a straight answer and a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) plan — on lots like these, new boards on tired framing is a bad bet twice over.
