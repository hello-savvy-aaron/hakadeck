## The town where the snow table changes

Monument sits at roughly 6,960 to 7,000 feet on the crest of the Palmer Divide, and that number matters more here than almost anywhere we build. The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department's snow-load criteria step up at exactly 7,000 feet — 43 psf ground snow below the line, 57 psf at or above it — and Monument straddles that line street by street. A deck framed for a Jackson Creek lot can be undersized for a Woodmoor one.

So the first thing we establish isn't the decking color. It's your elevation.

## Built for Palmer Divide weather

The Divide is a known upslope-snow hotspot — storms that brush Denver or Colorado Springs stack real depth here, and design wind runs 130–140 mph ultimate on top of it. That combination decides how we build: beams and footings sized to your lot's actual criteria, and [pergolas and patio covers](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) engineered for both snow bearing down and wind pulling up. A cover detailed for a sheltered Denver suburb is not the right structure for this ridge.

On the west side, where ponderosa forest runs up against neighborhoods, we also detail for embers — screened framing bays, and surfaces that don't invite fire.

## From Jackson Creek to King's Deer

Monument's housing runs from 1990s–2010s master-planned streets in Jackson Creek and newer builds in Sanctuary Pointe to wooded custom homes around Woodmoor and acreage lots in King's Deer. The newer subdivisions mostly need builder-grade decks replaced with something sized for the setting; the custom side needs structures designed around trees, slopes, and views.

Both get the same material logic. At this altitude the UV is relentless, the freeze-thaw cycling is sharp, and Monument sits squarely in the hail corridor — which is why we lead with [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) that shrugs off all three, and are candid in our [materials comparison](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) about where [wood](/services/hardwood-cedar-decks) still makes sense. Where the lot has a view of the Front Range, slim aluminum or cable [railing](/services/railings) keeps it.

## Repair, replace, and the honest answer

Plenty of Monument decks were framed to lighter assumptions than this ridge deserves. Before we quote anything, we look at the structure — ledger, posts, footings. If the bones are sound, a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite costs meaningfully less than a teardown. If the framing was never right for a 57 psf lot, we say so and quote a proper [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) on current-code structure. Either way you get the answer in writing.

## Permits through PPRBD

Building permits here don't come from Town Hall — they come from the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department, which serves Monument, Palmer Lake, and unincorporated El Paso County under the 2023 regional code. It's a different counter and a different submittal than the Denver-metro jurisdictions, including a dedicated residential deck e-review process. We prepare the drawings, file through PPRBD, and run inspections through final sign-off; town zoning and any HOA review get handled alongside. Our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the general process.
