## The far end of the Front Range run

El Paso County is Colorado's most populous county and the southern end of our range — down I-25 past the Palmer Divide, through the fast-growing Tri-Lakes area around Monument, and into Colorado Springs. The housing skews newer than the Denver suburbs: whole subdivisions in northern Colorado Springs and the Tri-Lakes corridor have gone up in the last two decades, many of them shipped with builder-grade wood decks, and many at elevations where snow and wind are structural facts rather than footnotes.

## Where we build in El Paso County

[Monument](/locations/monument) is the heart of our El Paso County work — Tri-Lakes subdivisions sitting right on the Palmer Divide, where elevation drives the engineering. [Colorado Springs](/locations/colorado-springs) is the anchor at the south end, from established Rockrimmon and Broadmoor-area homes to the new neighborhoods spreading north toward the county line. Between and around the two, we cover the I-25 corridor.

## Permits: one regional counter

Permitting here is refreshingly consolidated. The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department issues residential permits regionally — Colorado Springs, Monument, Palmer Lake, and unincorporated El Paso County all file through PPRBD under the 2023 Pikes Peak Regional Building Code. After working across a dozen separate metro-Denver building departments, one counter with one rulebook is a genuine simplification, and we know its habits. Drawings, submittal, inspections, corrections: all part of the build, as our [process](/process) page lays out.

## The 7,000-foot line

The number that matters most in this county is elevation. PPRBD's snow-load criteria step up at 7,000 feet — 43/30 psf below the line, 57/40 psf at or above it — and Monument and the Palmer Divide neighborhoods straddle exactly that boundary. A [covered deck or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) on the high side of the line carries meaningfully more load, which means bigger beams, closer posts, and deeper pockets of concrete. We check the tier for your site before drawing, and the structure is sized to the number, not to habit.

## Wind, sun, and new subdivisions

The Divide funnels real wind across this county, and open new-build lots feel all of it — uplift on covered structures is engineered here, not assumed away. The same openness means sun: young Tri-Lakes subdivisions have little mature shade, so built shade earns its keep fast. And because so much of the housing is newer, the most common project is upgrading a minimum-spec builder deck to an engineered [composite deck](/services/composite-decks) that handles hail, UV, and freeze-thaw with soap and water. Where an existing frame is sound, a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) is the honest quote; where it is not, we price a proper [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) and say why.

## An hour down the highway

From our shop in [Centennial](/locations/centennial), Monument is about fifty minutes and Colorado Springs an hour. We batch El Paso County site visits and plan build weeks accordingly — that is the whole effect of the distance. Price and [warranty](/warranty) do not change with the mile marker.
