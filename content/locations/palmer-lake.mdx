## The top of the Divide

Palmer Lake sits at about 7,300 feet at the crest of the Palmer Divide, an 1880s resort village of cabins and Victorian cottages on steep, rocky hillside lots, with newer custom homes clinging to the slopes above the lake. The Glen Park area, the old townsite, the Highway 105 corridor, and the hillsides under Ben Lomand Mountain and the Palmer Lake Star all share the same two facts: the ground is steep and rocky, and the sky delivers more snow here than anywhere between Denver and Colorado Springs.

Those two facts are the design brief. We engineer for the elevation your lot actually sits at, not for a Front Range average.

## Two conversations, one permit

The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department issues the building permit and runs the inspections — the same regional counter we file with in [Monument](/locations/monument) and [Colorado Springs](/locations/colorado-springs). PPRBD's homeowner permit list names decks, composite included, as permit-required work, and the department warns that unpermitted work can be recorded as a lien with the El Paso County Clerk.

The Town of Palmer Lake is the first conversation, though. Zoning and setback questions on these tight hillside lots go through Town Hall at 42 Valley Crescent, and on a steep parcel the setback answer often decides the deck's footprint before any structural drawing exists. We handle the town conversation, then prepare and file the PPRBD packet and manage inspections through final. Our [El Paso County hub](/locations/el-paso-county) covers the regional system, and our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) the general process.

## Fifty-seven pounds per square foot

PPRBD's design criteria step up at 7,000 feet, and Palmer Lake is above the line: ground snow load is 57 psf and flat-roof snow load 40 psf, against 43 and 30 below it. That is the number that sizes every beam and post under a [covered deck or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers), and on the Divide it is earned every spring. Frost depth along the Front Range runs 30–36 inches, and the town's historic hillside lots often require engineered footings in rock — which is a structural design job, not a post-hole digger job. An existing deck framed to a lower tier is the most common thing we find on inspection here.

## Decks that earn the view

On slopes this steep, an elevated deck is often the best square footage on the property, and the budget's center of gravity sits in the structure below it: taller posts, stepped footings, hardware sized for the load. Slim aluminum or cable railing keeps the lake and the Divide in view rather than caging it. Access on these lots is often tight, so we plan staging and equipment before the first delivery rather than discovering the driveway on build day. We lead with [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) because altitude UV, deep freeze-thaw cycling, heavy spring snow, and summer hail are exactly what it was built for; where cedar or hardwood still fits, we say so.

## Cottage-era decks, current-code answers

Many Palmer Lake decks predate the 57 psf tier by decades. We inspect the frame before quoting: sound structure earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite; an undersized frame or footings that have shifted on rock get a replacement recommendation on current-code structure. The inspection is free, and so is the verdict.
