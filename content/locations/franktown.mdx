## Ranch country, twenty minutes out

Franktown is what the south metro looked like before the master plans arrived: custom and ranch homes on five-plus acres, horse-friendly zoning, ponderosa woodland breaking into open ranch land, and Castlewood Canyon's rimrock as the local landmark. Neighborhoods like Russellville — five- and six-acre equine properties — Deerfield, and Bannockburn set the scale, with The Pradera just over the Parker line.

Decks here aren't suburban decks. The lots are bigger, the houses are custom, and the design brief usually starts with a view across your own land.

## Acreage changes the design

On five acres, a 12-by-16 platform looks lost. Franktown projects tend toward wraparounds, [covered sections](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) that hold up in wind and shade the afternoon side, and [outdoor kitchens](/services/outdoor-kitchens) that make the deck the working center of the property. Horse owners usually want the sightline to the barn kept open, which slim aluminum or cable [railing](/services/railings) preserves where bulky pickets would wall it off.

At 6,200 feet the material math is familiar Colorado: hard UV, freeze-thaw cycling, and hail. [Capped composite](/services/composite-decks) handles all three with soap and water; our [materials comparison](/composite-vs-hardwood-decking-colorado) lays out honestly where [wood](/services/hardwood-cedar-decks) still competes.

## Building ember-aware in the ponderosa

There's a reason the Colorado State Forest Service keeps a field office in Franktown: this is forested country, and the wildland-urban interface isn't an abstraction here. A deck is one of the most ember-exposed parts of a house — embers land on the surface, drift into framing bays, and find whatever's stored underneath.

On wooded lots we build accordingly by default: ignition-resistant surfaces rather than untreated softwood, framing bays closed or screened, and an underside that isn't designed as storage. It doesn't make a deck fireproof, and we won't claim it does. It makes it meaningfully less of a liability.

## Old decks on good houses

Plenty of Franktown homes carry decks that went up with the house decades ago. Before quoting, we inspect the structure — the ledger connection especially, because that's where aging decks fail expensively. Sound bones earn a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in composite for far less than starting over; a finished frame gets a straight [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) recommendation. Our [inspection guide](/deck-safety-inspection-guide) shows what we check.

## One permit counter, handled

Franktown is unincorporated, so permits are unambiguous: the Douglas County Building Division, full stop. County frost depth of 30–36 inches sets the footings, and the structural package goes through county review. We prepare, file, and manage inspections through sign-off — the same process we run constantly in [Castle Rock](/locations/castle-rock) and [Parker](/locations/parker). The [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the general background.
