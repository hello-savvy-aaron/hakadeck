    a: "Capped composite runs about $40-$70 per square foot installed, so a typical 300-400 square foot deck lands around $15,000-$30,000 with railing and stairs.   - q: "Why do footings matter so much in Weld County?"
    a: "Because of what is under the lawn. Much of the county sits on expansive clay soils that swell when wet and shrink when dry, heaving anything with shallow footings out of level over a few seasons. We drill caisson footings to full frost depth on every build — invisible on day one, and the difference between a level deck and a rebuilt one ten years later. It is the least glamorous line item on the quote and one of the most important."
  - q: "Do you really serve the whole county?"
    a: "The populated southwest corner, yes — the I-25 corridor towns from Erie and Firestone up through Frederick, Mead, and Johnstown to Windsor and Greeley, roughly forty to seventy minutes from our Centennial shop. We route Weld County builds together so crews work the corridor in blocks, which shapes our calendar rather than your price or warranty. Weld is a huge county; for addresses far out on the eastern plains, we will be honest about whether a site is inside our practical range."

## The state's new-build corridor

Weld County's southwest corner is where Colorado is growing fastest: a run of master-planned towns up the I-25 corridor between Denver and Fort Collins, most of their housing built in the last fifteen years, nearly all of it detached single-family on open high-plains ground. It is a county of nearly new houses — and of backyards that shipped with a concrete stoop or the minimum code deck and nothing else.

## Where we build in Weld County

Each town has its own page and its own detail: [Greeley](/locations/greeley), the county seat and its largest city; [Windsor](/locations/windsor), the boomtown of lakes-and-golf master plans; [Firestone](/locations/firestone) and [Frederick](/locations/frederick), the fast-growing Carbon Valley neighbors; [Mead](/locations/mead), small but adding rooftops quickly; [Johnstown](/locations/johnstown), spread along the I-25 growth seam; and [Erie](/locations/erie), whose Weld-side neighborhoods share the same open-prairie exposure as the rest of the corridor.

## The builder-basic backyard

The signature Weld County project is the first real deck on a nearly new house. Production builders deliver the house; the outdoor space is an afterthought, undersized and unshaded. Starting from close to nothing is genuinely the easier build — we design the [composite deck](/services/composite-decks), [railing](/services/railings), and shade as one plan, with no bad structure to work around, and set the footings right the first time. Many of these neighborhoods run on metro districts, and design review is standard in the newer master plans — that approval often drives the schedule more than the permit, so we sequence both submittals together.

## Hail, wind, and clay

Three physical facts shape every Weld County build. The county sits squarely in hail alley, which is the heart of the composite-first argument: capped composite shrugs off impact and altitude UV that leave softwood checked and gray in a few seasons. The open plains give wind a long run at every lot, so a [pergola or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) here is engineered for uplift, not catalog loads. And much of the county sits on expansive clay soils that heave shallow footings — the reason we drill caisson footings to full frost depth on every project.

## Permits, town by town

The towns issue their own permits, each its own way: Windsor through Community Development with SAFEbuilt inspections, Firestone through its Building Division with SAFEbuilt, Frederick through a Building Department that lists deck and porch as a permit type, Mead online-only through CommunityConnect with SAFEbuilt, Johnstown with a dedicated deck permit type in its eTRAKiT portal, and Erie for its Weld-side addresses. Unincorporated addresses file with Weld County. We identify the right counter for your parcel, file, and manage inspections through final — our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the general shape of the process.
