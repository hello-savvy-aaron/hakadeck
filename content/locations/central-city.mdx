## The richest square mile, stacked on a hillside

Central City earned the name "Richest Square Mile on Earth" in the gold years, and the housing still reads that way: 1870s brick and frame Victorians stacked up steep hillside lots on Lawrence Street, in Prosser Gulch, and out toward Nevadaville, above the casinos on Main and Eureka. Newer homes sit on forested acreage along the Central City Parkway corridor. At 8,510 feet, with rock close under the surface and slopes on nearly every lot, a deck here is a structural project first and an outdoor room second. This is also the Gilpin County seat, so the county courthouse is a few blocks from City Hall.

## Permits: SAFEbuilt through Community Core, after the HPC

The City of Central City Planning & Community Services runs permitting, with SAFEbuilt as the Building Official. Applications, fees, and inspection requests all go through the Community Core portal. The sequence is what trips people up: Central City is a National Historic Landmark District, and any exterior change or new construction in the district needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the six-member Historic Preservation Commission before the building permit is issued. We prepare the HPC packet — drawings, materials, and colors — then file the permit and run inspections through final. Parcels outside city limits answer to [Gilpin County](/locations/gilpin-county) instead; we confirm which counter yours is before anything else.

## Engineered, not prescriptive

The city does not publish its own design-load table, so the working reference is Gilpin County, which requires site-specific snow and wind loads from an ASCE Hazard Tool report and an engineered structural design on every permit. At 8,500 feet that is the right instinct anyway: snow stacks for months, the frost line is deep, and freeze-thaw works every fastener. We engineer beams, posts, and footings to the figures for your parcel, and a [covered deck or patio cover](/services/pergolas-patio-covers) is designed as the roof it is.

## Wildfire code, confirmed per parcel

Gilpin County has adopted the 2024 International Wildland-Urban Interface Code and the 2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code; the city's own adoption status is something we confirm with Planning & Community Services for each project. Either way, we treat ignition-resistant decking as the baseline on forested Central City lots: [capped composite](/services/composite-decks) or mineral-based boards, framing bays closed or screened against embers, and an underside that is not a woodpile. On a historic-district home, we pick a board profile and color the HPC will accept.

## Hillside decks that earn the view

On Lawrence Street and in Prosser Gulch the lots drop away fast, and an elevated deck is often the best flat space on the property — with the budget's center of gravity in the structure below. We design from the actual grade and choose railing that keeps the view down the gulch open; on a Victorian, that often means a traditional profile the commission will approve rather than cable.

## Repair versus replace

Plenty of Central City decks date to earlier snow-load and hardware assumptions a reviewer would not accept today. We inspect the frame and answer plainly: sound structure earns a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) in ignition-resistant composite, aged-out structure gets a [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) plan on engineered framing. The answer comes in writing either way.
