## Flat lots, new houses, frequent moves

Fountain is a city of about 31,000 people at 5,545 feet, directly south of Colorado Springs and next door to Fort Carson. Outside the older Main Street core, the housing is 1990s–2020s production homes on flat lots — Cross Creek, Heritage, Countryside, Mesa Ridge, Fountain Valley Ranch — and the military turnover means those homes change hands more often than most Front Range subdivisions. A lot of them trade with the builder-grade wood deck they were shipped with, which is the most common thing we are asked to fix here.

## Two steps: City Planning, then PPRBD

Fountain's permit is a two-step process, and the city runs the first step itself. Plot and zoning approval comes from the City of Fountain Planning Department, on the first floor of City Hall at 116 South Main Street, before the regional permit is filed. Then the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department reviews the plans, issues the building permit, and performs the inspections — the same regional counter that covers [Colorado Springs](/locations/colorado-springs) and nearly all of [El Paso County](/locations/el-paso-county), with decks, composite included, on its permit-required list.

We handle the Planning step, prepare one clean PPRBD packet, and manage inspections through final. Our [permit guide](/deck-permits-south-denver-metro) covers the general process, and a fall contract gets ahead of the spring permit queue.

## Wind first, then snow

Fountain is lower, warmer, and windier than Colorado Springs. PPRBD's design criteria below 7,000 feet set ground snow at 43 psf and flat-roof snow at 30 psf, and that number sizes the beams and posts under any [covered deck or pergola](/services/pergolas-patio-covers). But the daily fact of life on these open, flat lots is wind: there is nothing to slow it down, and a roofed structure is loaded upward in a storm. Engineered post bases, hardware, and footings below the 30–36-inch frost line are what keep a cover attached out here, and we do not pick them from a catalog.

The same openness means sun. Young subdivisions have little mature shade, so a covered section or a pergola earns its keep fast; the [covered deck cost guide](/blog/covered-deck-cost-denver) has honest numbers.

## The builder-deck upgrade

The typical Fountain project is a minimum-spec wood deck swapped for an engineered [composite deck](/services/composite-decks) that handles hail, UV, and freeze-thaw with soap and water instead of a stain schedule. Flat lots keep the structure simple, which keeps many builds in the lower to middle part of the range, and the money goes where you see it: a wider footprint, a cover, railing that suits the house. Railing is usually the second upgrade: the wood pickets that came with the house swapped for slim aluminum that needs a rinse instead of a refinish. Where cedar or hardwood still fits, we say so.

Because so many of these homes are newer, the frame is often sound. When it is, we say so and quote a [re-deck](/services/deck-repair) at $20–$35 per square foot over the existing structure. When the ledger or posts are undersized, we price a proper [replacement](/services/deck-replacement) and explain why. Either answer comes in writing, and a free structural inspection comes first.
